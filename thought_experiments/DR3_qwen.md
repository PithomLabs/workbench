# Constructive Falsification of Invariant Set Theory: Can a Dyadic Toy Model Derive the Quantum Potential?

## Strategic Pivot to Constructive Falsification

The current research phase represents a decisive strategic pivot away from broad theoretical surveys toward a focused, constructive falsification program . Having identified the central unresolved questions of Fisher selection and ℏ-universality, the primary objective is no longer to survey possibilities but to rigorously test a specific hypothesis: whether a well-defined, minimal deterministic system with an IST-like geometric structure can spontaneously generate the Fisher information functional and its universal coefficient without these elements being pre-inserted or engineered into the model's definition . This approach is driven by the recognition that abstract theorizing has reached a point of diminishing returns; the critical next step is to make the mathematics produce a definitive answer—or fail to produce one . The new methodology follows a strict, four-step process: `candidate structure → minimal explicit model → exact calculation → survives/fails` . This framework is fundamentally falsificationist in spirit. The value of the research is not contingent on a positive result but on the clarity of the outcome. A negative result, demonstrating that the proposed mechanism fails, is equally valuable as it would rule out a specific class of explanations and force a necessary re-evaluation of the underlying theory. This marks a mature stage of the project, where the focus shifts to producing a mathematically rigorous verdict on the possibility of emergent quantum structure from deterministic foundations .

This strategic shift is informed by recent findings which have weakened the prospects of alternative targets, such as deriving the osmotic velocity microscopically from Nelsonian stochastic processes . It is now clear that the primary target should be the emergence of the Fisher/Dirichlet Hamiltonian term directly from deterministic microscopic dynamics . While an effective osmotic velocity may still appear later as a macroscopic representation, building the theory around obtaining it at the microscopic level is deemed the wrong path forward . The new experimental design centers on a single, cleanly defined question: given a deterministic dynamical system on a fractal state space, does its continuum limit under a legitimate coarse-graining procedure produce an energy functional proportional to $\int |\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^2dx$, and if so, does the proportionality constant naturally take the universal form $\hbar^2/2m$? This question provides a sharp, unambiguous criterion for success or failure, avoiding the ambiguities inherent in more qualitative investigations . The research architecture is therefore structured to provide a decisive answer to this foundational query, which serves as the true bridge between IST's ontological postulates and the mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics .

## Investigating IST-Native Mechanisms for Fisher Selection

The investigation into Fisher selection is conducted through a two-tiered approach, designed to first explore IST-native mechanisms and then use general deterministic analogues as discovery tools . The primary target, Tier 1, is to determine if the intrinsic mathematical properties of IST—the p-adic geometry, symbolic dynamics, and fractal scaling of the invariant set—are sufficient to select the Fisher information functional. The natural metric for describing distances on a fractal set like the Cantor set is the p-adic metric, a non-Archimedean metric with ultrametric properties distinct from Euclidean geometry [[2,5,8]]. This suggests that any continuum limit derived from such a structure may inherit novel geometric and scaling properties that could naturally lead to the Dirichlet energy, which is quadratic in gradients [[46]]. The topology of the p-adic integers, homeomorphic to the Cantor set, provides a robust geometric foundation upon which dynamics can be built [[2,6]].

A key proposal within this tier is to define a discrete information quantity on the branches of the Cantor set's self-similar structure and investigate its continuum limit . Symbolic dynamics provides a language to describe trajectories on the invariant set as infinite sequences of symbols representing visits to different branches [[409,425]]. By defining a discrete energy or information measure based on these symbolic sequences and their probabilities under the system's deterministic map, one can probe whether this discrete quantity converges to the Fisher information functional in the coarse-grained limit [[48,49]]. The interplay between the deterministic map and the unique invariant measure, often the Haar measure for p-adic systems, is crucial, as it governs how probability mass is distributed across the fractal branches and thus determines the emergent macroscopic density $\rho(x)$ [[125,393]]. The self-similar scaling of the Cantor set imposes constraints on the possible form of the limiting energy functional, making the quadratic nature of the Dirichlet energy a plausible, albeit non-trivial, outcome [[7]].

As a secondary line of inquiry, Tier 2 explores general deterministic systems outside of IST to discover candidate selection mechanisms that IST could potentially instantiate . These systems serve as benchmarks for understanding how complex macroscopic behavior can emerge from simple microscopic rules. Chaotic homogenization, for instance, studies how microscopic fluctuations average out to produce an effective macroscopic description with constant coefficients, providing a powerful mathematical framework for coarse-graining [[110,111]]. Fast-slow systems offer another perspective on how collective dynamics emerge from disparate time scales [[164]]. Perhaps most relevant are Vlasov-type theories and the related McKean-Vlasov equations, which describe the mean-field limit of large systems of interacting particles [[43,478]]. These models demonstrate how a self-consistent macroscopic equation can arise from a distribution-dependent force law, a feature analogous to the self-referential nature of the quantum potential [[472,477]]. By studying these analogues, one can better understand the conditions under which a specific functional form like the Fisher information might be selected, providing a context against which to evaluate the plausibility of an IST-native derivation [[42,160]].

## The Challenge of ħ-Universality: From Engineering to Geometric Fixing

Once a mechanism for generating the Fisher term is established, the second major challenge is explaining the origin of the universal coefficient $\hbar^2/2m$. The requirement is to derive this specific form from the system's intrinsic properties, not by engineering it into the model . A common approach in statistical physics is the Green-Kubo formula, which expresses transport coefficients as time integrals of microscopic correlation functions [[272,273]]. However, this serves primarily as a falsification baseline . The resulting effective coefficient depends critically on the detailed microscopic dynamics and correlations, creating a severe universality problem [[105]]. For the Green-Kubo mechanism to yield $D(m) = \hbar/(2m)$, it would require a highly specific, non-generic relationship that produces both the universal $1/m$ dependence and the same value of $\hbar$ across all physical systems. Given that $\hbar$ is a fundamental constant of nature, not a material property, this seems unlikely, suggesting that a generic dynamical correlation mechanism is insufficient to explain its universality .

The more promising path lies within IST's native toolkit, focusing on mechanisms rooted in number theory, scale invariance, and p-adic geometry . The ultimate goal is to find a derivation of $\hbar$ that avoids dimensional analysis, which is forbidden as a valid derivation . The fractal, scale-invariant nature of the invariant set offers a potential route to fixing a fundamental action scale [[66,69]]. Just as the Planck length emerges from a combination of the gravitational constant G, the speed of light c, and the conventional Planck constant $\hbar$, the fundamental scale in IST could emerge from the intrinsic geometric and arithmetic properties of the p-adic numbers [[316,452]]. The p-adic integers themselves are constructed as formal power series, embodying a discrete, hierarchical structure that underlies the continuous manifold in the limit [[449]]. The transition from this discrete, p-adic substrate to a continuum description involves a renormalization group-like flow, where parameters change with the scale of observation [[232,364]]. The fixed points of this flow could potentially define the universal constants of physics, including $\hbar$.

This idea is supported by work in p-adic mathematical physics and TGD (Topological Geometrodynamics), where p-adic thermodynamics is used to calculate particle masses and other physical quantities from first principles [[127,128,200]]. Extending this framework to derive $\hbar$ itself remains a significant open problem, but it represents the most viable direction for a truly emergent explanation of quantum universality within the IST paradigm . The connection between the discrete symmetries of the p-adic lattice and the continuous gauge symmetries of standard physics is a key area of exploration, suggesting that the universal constants are not free parameters but are determined by the deep geometric fabric of reality as described by IST [[208,209]]. This pursuit moves beyond mere parameter fitting and aims for a genuine derivation grounded in the theory's core postulates.

## Analytical Framework for Emergent Continuum Dynamics

To analyze the transition from the discrete, deterministic dynamics of the toy model to the continuum functional, a sophisticated analytical framework is required. The central mathematical tool for this purpose is Γ-convergence, a notion of convergence for functionals that ensures the convergence of their minimizers [[19,188]]. The primary analytical task is to prove that the sequence of discrete Dirichlet energy functionals, $E_N[\rho_N]$, defined on the graph approximations of the Cantor set, Γ-converges to a limiting continuum functional $E_{eff}[\rho]$ [[215]]. If successful, this would formally establish that the macroscopic energy is described by the limit functional, thereby answering the first part of the research question.

Large deviations theory provides a complementary and powerful framework for understanding the emergence of macroscopic descriptions from microscopic stochastic or deterministic dynamics [[179,248]]. The theory quantifies the probability of rare events and shows that, for many systems, the probability of observing a certain macrostate follows a large deviation principle with an exponential dependence on an action or rate functional [[178,245]]. In many cases of interest, this rate functional takes the form of a gradient flow driven by a specific energy functional [[305,306]]. The relative Fisher information often appears as this rate functional, linking the probabilistic concept of large deviations directly to the information-theoretic quantity of interest [[174,222]]. Therefore, analyzing the large deviation properties of the trajectories of the dyadic system on the Cantor set could provide an alternative route to deriving the Fisher information as the natural energy functional governing the system's long-term behavior [[176]].

Furthermore, the theory of Dirichlet forms offers a robust probabilistic-analytic bridge between the discrete dynamics and the continuum PDEs they generate [[15,40]]. A Dirichlet form can be associated with the generator of the stochastic process corresponding to the dynamics. In the discrete setting, this corresponds to a quadratic form on the graph of the Cantor set approximation. The study of traces of Dirichlet forms onto lower-dimensional sets or fractals is a well-established field, and it provides techniques for understanding how the energy dissipates and how the continuum limit is approached [[112,484,485]]. By formulating the microscopic energy as a discrete Dirichlet form, one can leverage this extensive mathematical machinery to analyze its convergence properties and identify the characteristics of the limiting continuum form. This multi-pronged analytical approach, combining Γ-convergence, large deviations, and Dirichlet forms, provides a comprehensive toolkit for rigorously investigating the emergence of the Fisher information functional from the proposed deterministic toy model.
