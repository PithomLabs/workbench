This is a genuinely well-engineered research plan — the axiom firewall in WP0, the explicit non-negotiable rule that WP1's Fisher result gets cited rather than re-derived as novel, and the closing instinct ("try very hard to prove the survivor space empty before building anything") are all exactly the discipline this whole investigation has been reaching for. A few things worth pressure-testing before it becomes the operative plan.

**T2.4 needs to inherit last round's correction more carefully than it currently does.** The wording — "if the microscopic Koopman system is weakly mixing, then an injective exact linear intertwiner cannot embed a Schrödinger sector containing nontrivial bound-state eigenvectors into the Koopman point spectrum" — is the *correct*, narrowly-scoped version, and the document is right to insist "that distinction is now permanent." But it's worth being precise about what this leaves open for WP3.3: bound states correspond to point spectrum, but scattering states in ordinary quantum mechanics correspond to absolutely continuous spectrum. A weakly mixing system that's excluded from embedding *point* spectrum might still, in principle, have room to embed an absolutely-continuous or singular-continuous sector matching a Hamiltonian's continuous spectrum. If WP3.3 is meant to search singular-continuous territory specifically, T2.4's proof needs a companion statement about what happens to *non-eigenvector* (continuous-spectrum) embeddings under weak mixing — otherwise the no-go and the survivor-search are talking about different spectral types and might not actually meet in the middle the way the plan assumes.

**WP2.2's Fisher-monotonicity argument, reused here as the concrete obstruction for the no-go paper, needs the same generalization flagged for WP3's search two turns ago.** The $dI_F/dt \le 0$ result was verified for linear diffusion (the heat-equation case). Formalizing it as a blanket "Markov/diffusive limits cannot supply conserved quantum structure" claim in Paper B needs the argument extended to the actual Fokker-Planck equations that come out of deterministic homogenization — those generally include a drift term alongside diffusion, and drift transport doesn't obviously share the same monotonicity property the pure heat-equation computation established. This is very likely still true (transport alone is measure-preserving and shouldn't rescue a diffusive Fisher decrease), but "very likely true" and "formalized in a paper claiming class-wide rigidity" are different bars, and WP2.2 as written treats the heat-equation result as though it already covers the general Fokker-Planck case.

**WP3.2's Obligation L is the single highest-leverage item in the whole plan, and it's correctly identified as such — worth saying why explicitly.** If true, it means anyone who ever exhibits a complex linear lift with conserved modulus has *automatically* produced a Hamiltonian $(\rho,S)$ system, which by WP1's Fisher rigidity theorem automatically produces the Fisher term. That would collapse WP3's entire search from "find an amplitude realization AND separately verify it's Hamiltonian AND separately get Fisher" down to "find an amplitude realization" — one condition instead of three. That's worth flagging as the plan's real fulcrum: proving or killing Obligation L should arguably happen *before* WP3.1's broader survey of nonlinear-lift mathematics, not roughly concurrently with it, since a proof of L changes what WP3.1 even needs to look for.

**A gap the plan doesn't name: WP2's four no-go routes (factor maps, diffusive limits, conditional averaging, exact linear Koopman intertwiners) may not be jointly exhaustive of "obvious" mechanisms, and the plan should say so rather than implying completeness once WP2 closes.** Missing at minimum: cocycle-twisted lifts and projective-representation constructions are listed in WP3.1's survey as candidate *survivors* to investigate, but nothing in WP2 rules out the more mundane possibility of a *nonlinear* averaging scheme that isn't captured by T2.3's "conditional averaging ⇒ contractive" argument — e.g., an averaging operation weighted by a dynamically-evolving measure rather than the fixed fiber measure T2.3 presumably assumes. If T2.3's no-go is proved only for fixed-measure conditional expectations, that should be flagged as a scope limit the same way T2.4's scope was pinned down, rather than silently generalized to "conditional averaging" as a whole in the later summary sections.

**One structural question about sequencing that the plan's own logic raises but doesn't resolve.** The execution order runs WP2 (no-go machinery) and WP4 (topology/action unit) in parallel, then into WP3. But WP4's search for a $U(1)$ bundle or integral connection on $I_U$ is presumably being conducted on the *same* invariant-set structure WP2 is simultaneously trying to prove can't support a linear amplitude at all. If WP2 succeeds in closing off the "obvious" routes, does that constrain what kind of topological structure WP4 should even be looking for — should WP4's search for $\kappa$ be conditioned on whatever survives WP2, rather than run as a fully independent parallel track? Right now WP4 reads as topology-hunting in the abstract, disconnected from whatever specific residual mechanism WP2 and WP3 converge on. Given the plan's own later insight — WP3 and WP4 "need to run together because the phase/action unit may be part of the amplitude realization itself" — it would strengthen the plan to make that coupling explicit from WP4's start rather than introducing it only in the closing remark.

**Net assessment:** this is executable, appropriately skeptical of its own prior enthusiasm, and correctly organized around trying to kill the hypothesis before building on it. The scope-precision discipline that made T2.4's wording trustworthy needs to be applied with equal rigor to T2.2 and T2.3's generalizations, and Obligation L deserves to be moved earlier in the queue given how much downstream work its resolution would either collapse or redirect.
