Yes. In fact, **I think the mathematical route should come first**.

The toy model should be a later diagnostic, not the primary research method.

The reason is that our real question is not:

> “Can we find one deterministic system that happens to produce Fisher information?”

It is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Is Fisher/quantum structure mathematically forced by the deterministic IST assumptions?}}
$$

That is a much more general question, and a toy model cannot establish it.

### What mathematics can do instead

We can formulate the problem as a **necessity/sufficiency theorem**.

Start with the strongest assumptions we are actually willing to grant IST:

$$
(I_U,\Phi_t,\mu,\pi)
$$

and ask what effective macroscopic structures are compatible with them.

We then impose only properties that are independently motivated, such as:

$$
\text{locality},\quad
\text{conservation},\quad
\text{Galilean invariance},\quad
\text{time reversibility/Hamiltonian structure},
$$

together with the existence of a coarse complex amplitude

$$
\Phi=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{iS/\kappa}.
$$

Then classify the possible local functionals

$$
E[\rho,S]
=
\int
\mathcal H(\rho,S,\nabla\rho,\nabla S,\ldots)\,dx.
$$

The key mathematical question becomes:

> **Are the IST-compatible symmetries and structural constraints sufficient to force the Fisher term?**

If yes, we obtain something like

$$
E_q[\rho]
=
\frac{\kappa^2}{8m}
\int
\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho}\,dx
$$

as a **uniqueness theorem**, rather than as the output of an example.

That would be vastly more powerful.

## The route I would take

There are really four mathematical gates.

### Gate 1 — Classify the admissible macroscopic Hamiltonians

Assume the coarse dynamics admits a Hamiltonian formulation for density and phase:

$$
(\rho,S)
$$

with

$$
E[\rho,S]
=
\int
\left[
\frac{\rho|\nabla S|^2}{2m}
+\rho V
+F(\rho,\nabla\rho)
\right]dx.
$$

Then derive the most general \(F\) compatible with the required symmetries.

We already know something important here: **Fisher is not unique merely because it is a Dirichlet form.** Claude correctly noted that “Dirichlet form” alone is too weak.

So we need stronger constraints.

---

### Gate 2 — Demand linear complex evolution

Define

$$
\psi=\sqrt{\rho}e^{iS/\kappa}.
$$

Now impose that the resulting field equation be linear:

$$
i\kappa\partial_t\psi
=
\left(
-\frac{\kappa^2}{2m}\Delta+V
\right)\psi.
$$

Then the nonlinear terms generated by the Madelung transform determine the allowable \(F\).

This is where the known Reginatto/Hall–Reginatto mathematics becomes relevant.

The outcome is essentially:

$$
F(\rho,\nabla\rho)
\propto
\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho}.
$$

So this part does **not** need a toy model.

It can be established analytically.

But remember the crucial limitation:

$$
\boxed{
\text{linearity}\Rightarrow\text{Fisher}
}
$$

is not the same as

$$
\boxed{
\text{IST}\Rightarrow\text{linearity}.
}
$$

The latter is our unresolved problem.

---

## Gate 3 — Derive linearity from deterministic dynamics

This is the really interesting mathematical challenge.

Instead of constructing a toy system, ask whether deterministic dynamics possessing an invariant-set structure can naturally induce a closed observable algebra

$$
\mathcal A
$$

such that

$$
U_t\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal A,
$$

where

$$
U_t f=f\circ\Phi_t
$$

is the Koopman operator.

Then we search for a subspace \(\mathcal H_{\rm eff}\subset L^2(I_U,\mu)\) where

$$
U_t|_{\mathcal H_{\rm eff}}
=
e^{-itH/\kappa}
$$

and determine whether

$$
H
=
-\frac{\kappa^2}{2m}\Delta+V
$$

can arise as the effective generator.

This is much deeper than “build a toy model.”

It asks for a **representation theorem**:

$$
\boxed{
\text{deterministic IST dynamics}
\Rightarrow
\text{effective Hilbert-space representation}
}
$$

with the Schrödinger generator as a particular emergent representation.

Koopman theory gives us a mathematically legitimate starting point for this because deterministic nonlinear dynamics already admits a linear evolution on observables. The unresolved issue is whether IST supplies the **right invariant subspace, measure, locality and spectral structure**.

---

## Gate 4 — Derive \(\kappa=\hbar\)

Only after Gates 1–3 succeed do we attack the coefficient.

We should not ask:

> “Can we choose constants so that \(\kappa=\hbar\)?”

We should ask:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Does IST contain a mathematically unavoidable action quantum?}
}
$$

For example, can one prove that some invariant/topological quantity satisfies

$$
\oint dS = 2\pi n\kappa
$$

with \(\kappa\) fixed by the underlying structure?

Then mass dependence can potentially enter through the Hamiltonian rather than through the fundamental action unit.

This separates two very different questions:

$$
\text{shape of quantum correction}
$$

from

$$
\text{universal action scale}.
$$

That is much cleaner.

---

# And there is an even stronger possibility

Rather than trying to prove that Fisher emerges, we can attempt a **mathematical no-go theorem**.

Suppose we prove:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Any deterministic IST model satisfying A,B,C,D cannot generate}
\;
\frac{|\nabla\rho|^2}{\rho}
}
$$

unless it introduces some additional structure \(X\).

Then we have identified the missing axiom exactly.

That would be a major result even if the answer is negative.

Conversely, suppose we prove:

$$
A+B+C+D
\Longrightarrow
\text{unique local correction}=I_F.
$$

Then the Fisher term becomes a **theorem of the framework**, not a fitted feature.

That is precisely the level of result we should be aiming for.

---

# So what role remains for the toy model?

Very small.

A toy model becomes a **unit test for a theorem or mechanism**.

For example, once we conjecture

$$
\text{IST assumptions}
\Rightarrow
\text{singular-continuous Koopman sector}
\Rightarrow
\text{second-order effective generator},
$$

we can construct a minimal deterministic example and see whether each implication survives.

But we should **not use the toy model to discover Fisher by reverse-engineering its energy**.

Qwen's proposed model makes exactly that danger visible: it begins by choosing a discrete Dirichlet energy and then studies its Γ-limit. 

That is useful mathematics, but it isn't yet the foundational derivation we're after.

---

# I would therefore change the entire research program

Instead of:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Toy model}\rightarrow\text{Fisher?}
}
$$

make the principal program:

$$
\boxed{
\text{IST axioms}
\rightarrow
\text{admissible observable structures}
\rightarrow
\text{closure theorem}
\rightarrow
\text{linear amplitude}
\rightarrow
\text{Fisher uniqueness}
\rightarrow
\hbar\text{ universality}
}
$$

with toy models only as falsification tools.

And this makes Claude's singular-continuous-spectrum observation more valuable. It gives us a concrete mathematical branch to investigate inside the Koopman/spectral step rather than blindly constructing examples.
