# Where Complexity Theory Shines in Physics: Circuit Geometry, Holographic Duals, and Computational Hardness

## The organizing idea
Complexity theory does not shine in physics as a generator of continuous motion, a measure of spatial distance, or a simple counter of microstates. It shines in three specific mathematical structures: **circuit complexity (the geometry of quantum state space), holographic duality (the computational growth of black hole interiors), and computational hardness (physical limits of simulation and phase classification)**. Not all complicated systems, not all hard equations, not all entangled states — but a specific, identifiable subclass.

The dividing line is this: complexity is present when the physical structure is defined by the *computational resources* (gates, time, memory) required to prepare, simulate, or transform a state, and when this resource scaling reveals a fundamental physical principle. Without a notion of computational cost, gate counts, or complexity classes (P, NP, BQP, QMA), complexity theory is absent. With it, complexity provides the fine-grained, information-theoretic skeleton of quantum gravity and many-body physics.

Below: the three structures, the duality in each, and the class of physical systems where complexity is genuinely active.

### 1. Circuit Complexity and Quantum State Space (The Geometry of Entanglement)
**What it is**
The study of the minimum number of elementary operations (gates) required to prepare a specific quantum state from a trivial reference state. Unlike entanglement entropy, which measures *correlations*, circuit complexity measures *how hard it is to build* the state.

**When does a structure have complexity content?**
A generic quantum state is just a vector in Hilbert space. A structure has complexity content when the state is viewed as the output of a quantum circuit, and the minimal depth or gate count of that circuit becomes the primary physical observable.

**Four levels of complexity content in state spaces:**
*   **Level 1 — Tensor Network Depth.** The minimal bond dimension or layer depth of a Matrix Product State (MPS) or Projected Entangled Pair State (PEPS). This appears in: 1D gapped systems, area-law entanglement.
*   **Level 2 — Nielsen’s Geometry.** A continuous Riemannian metric defined on the unitary group, where the "distance" is the circuit complexity. This appears in: Continuous tensor networks, quantum control theory.
*   **Level 3 — Chaotic/Thermal Complexity.** The linear growth of circuit complexity in scrambling systems long after entanglement entropy has saturated. This appears in: SYK model, chaotic quantum circuits.
*   **Level 4 — Cosmological Complexity.** The complexity of the wavefunction of the universe during inflation. This appears in: de Sitter space, cosmological perturbations.

**The duality**
The duality is between the physical quantum state and the minimal computational cost.
| Physical concept | Complexity object | Duality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State preparation | Minimal gate count ($C_U$) | Nielsen's Finsler geometry |
| Entanglement saturation | Volume law vs. Area law | Tensor network bond dimension |
| Late-time dynamics | Linear growth of complexity | Scrambling beyond thermalization |
| Quantum error correction | Logical vs. physical gate depth | Fault-tolerant threshold |

**Where complexity shines**
*   **Strongest case: Late-time quantum dynamics.** Entanglement entropy saturates in time logarithmic to system size. Circuit complexity continues to grow linearly for exponentially long times. Complexity is the *only* observable that captures the late-time evolution of chaotic quantum systems.
*   **Second strongest: Tensor network renormalization.** The efficiency of simulating a physical state (via MERA or PEPS) is strictly dictated by its circuit complexity. Low complexity means the state is physically compressible.
*   **Third: Quantum phase transitions.** The divergence of circuit complexity (or its derivatives) can signal quantum phase transitions, even in systems where traditional order parameters fail.

**Where complexity is not active**
*   Integrable systems (where states can be prepared with low-depth circuits due to infinite conservation laws).
*   Simple thermal ensembles (where the state is maximally mixed, and complexity is undefined or trivial).
*   Classical continuous dynamics (where the concept of discrete gate counts does not apply).

### 2. Holographic Duality and Black Hole Interiors (The Gravity of Computation)
**What it is**
The conjecture that the computational complexity of the boundary quantum state in AdS/CFT is dual to a geometric property of the bulk spacetime, specifically the volume or action of the black hole interior.

**When does a structure have holographic complexity content?**
A generic black hole is described by its mass, charge, and spin (no-hair theorem). A structure has holographic complexity content when the *interior* geometry of the black hole (which is causally disconnected from the boundary) is identified with the computational complexity of the boundary state.

**Four types of holographic complexity:**
*   **Type 1 — Complexity = Volume (CV).** The complexity of the boundary state is proportional to the maximal spatial volume of the bulk Einstein-Rosen bridge. 
*   **Type 2 — Complexity = Action (CA).** The complexity is proportional to the gravitational action evaluated on the Wheeler-DeWitt patch of the bulk.
*   **Type 3 — Subsystem Complexity.** The complexity of a subregion of the boundary is dual to a localized geometric region in the bulk.
*   **Type 4 — Complexity = Nothing (Refined).** Recent proposals modifying the CV/CA conjectures to resolve inconsistencies at late times or for charged black holes.

**The duality**
The duality is between boundary computation and bulk geometry.
| Boundary concept | Bulk geometric object | Duality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circuit complexity | Maximal volume of Einstein-Rosen bridge | CV Conjecture ($C \sim V / G l$) |
| Circuit complexity | Gravitational action on WDW patch | CA Conjecture ($C \sim A / \pi \hbar$) |
| Entanglement entropy | Minimal surface area (RT/HRT formula) | Holographic entanglement |
| Information scrambling | Shockwave geometry / Butterfly effect | Chaos in the bulk |

**Where complexity shines**
*   **Strongest case: Black hole interior growth.** A black hole's event horizon area (entropy) stops growing once it reaches thermal equilibrium. But its interior volume continues to grow for exponentially long times. Holographic complexity explains this: the boundary state's complexity is still growing linearly, and the bulk geometry must grow to match it.
*   **Second strongest: The Second Law of Quantum Complexity.** Just as entropy gives the Second Law of Thermodynamics, the linear growth of complexity suggests a "Second Law of Quantum Complexity" that governs the irreversible growth of black hole interiors.
*   **Third: Resolving the Firewall Paradox.** Complexity provides a mechanism for the black hole interior to be smoothly connected (low complexity for infalling observers) while remaining highly scrambled (high complexity for boundary observers).

**Where complexity is not active**
*   Asymptotically flat spacetimes (where the holographic dictionary is not well-defined).
*   Small black holes (where quantum gravity corrections dominate and the semiclassical geometric dual breaks down).
*   Systems without a gravity dual (where the bulk geometric interpretation is absent).

### 3. Computational Hardness as Physical Law (The Limits of Simulation)
**What it is**
The use of computational complexity classes (P, NP, BQP, QMA) to define physical phases of matter, establish the limits of physical simulation, and protect physical phenomena (like cryptography) from being reversed or simulated.

**When does a structure have computational hardness content?**
A generic physical problem is just a differential equation or a Hamiltonian. A structure has hardness content when the problem of finding its ground state, simulating its dynamics, or sampling its output is proven to be complete for a specific complexity class, making it fundamentally intractable for certain classes of computers.

**Four types of computational hardness in physics:**
*   **Type 1 — QMA-Hardness.** Finding the ground state energy of a local Hamiltonian is QMA-complete (the quantum analogue of NP-complete). This appears in: Quantum spin glasses, frustrated magnets.
*   **Type 2 — BQP and Quantum Advantage.** Sampling from the output distribution of certain quantum systems is in BQP but believed to be outside the polynomial hierarchy (PH). This appears in: Boson sampling, random circuit sampling.
*   **Type 3 — Cryptographic Hardness.** Physical systems whose security relies on the computational hardness of lattice problems (e.g., Learning With Errors). This appears in: Quantum key distribution, post-quantum cryptography.
*   **Type 4 — Topological Protection as Hardness.** The stability of topological phases is reinterpreted as the computational hardness of creating logical errors (requiring non-local, high-depth circuits). This appears in: Toric code, surface codes.

**The duality**
The duality is between the physical Hamiltonian and the complexity class.
| Physical system | Complexity class | Physical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local Hamiltonian ground state | QMA-Complete | Fundamental intractability of quantum many-body physics |
| Boson sampling / Random circuits | #P-Hard / BQP | Quantum supremacy / advantage |
| Toric code / Surface code | BQP (with fault tolerance) | Topological protection = computational hardness of errors |
| Learning With Errors (LWE) | Worst-case lattice hardness | Information-theoretic security from physical noise |

**Where complexity shines**
*   **Strongest case: Quantum Supremacy/Advantage.** The proof that classical computers cannot efficiently simulate certain quantum systems (like random circuit sampling) is the most direct experimental application of computational complexity to physics. It establishes a hard physical limit on classical simulation.
*   **Second strongest: Quantum Error Correction.** The threshold theorem is fundamentally a complexity statement: as long as the physical noise rate is below a threshold, the *computational cost* (circuit depth) required for an error to corrupt the logical state grows exponentially. 
*   **Third: Spin Glasses and Optimization.** The physical behavior of quantum spin glasses is deeply linked to the QMA-hardness of the underlying Hamiltonian. The rugged energy landscape is a physical manifestation of computational hardness.

**Where complexity is not active**
*   Free fermion systems (which can be simulated efficiently in classical polynomial time, P).
*   1D gapped systems (where tensor networks provide efficient classical simulation).
*   Systems in thermal equilibrium (where the partition function can sometimes be approximated, though exact calculation remains #P-hard).

### 4. Where Circuit Geometry, Holography, and Hardness meet
The deepest case for complexity in physics is when state space geometry, holographic duality, and computational hardness all align simultaneously.

**Three examples:**
1.  **AdS/CFT and Quantum Error Correction:**
    *   *Circuit Geometry:* The boundary state is prepared by a low-depth circuit for local operators, but high-depth for bulk interior operators.
    *   *Holography:* The bulk geometry emerges from the entanglement and complexity of the boundary code.
    *   *Hardness:* The bulk locality is protected by the computational hardness of decoding the boundary state (holographic error correction).
    *   *Complexity structure:* Complete.
2.  **Black Hole Information Paradox:**
    *   *Circuit Geometry:* The scrambling of information is modeled by random quantum circuits (linear complexity growth).
    *   *Holography:* The interior volume grows to match the complexity; the Page curve is recovered via replica wormholes.
    *   *Hardness:* Decoding the Hawking radiation is computationally hard (exponential circuit depth), protecting the equivalence principle (no drama at the horizon).
    *   *Complexity structure:* Complete.
3.  **Topological Quantum Computation:**
    *   *Circuit Geometry:* Anyon braiding corresponds to specific, low-depth topological circuits.
    *   *Holography:* (In specific models like 3D gravity) The topological phase is dual to a bulk TQFT.
    *   *Hardness:* Simulating the anyon statistics classically is #P-hard, but executing them physically is fault-tolerant (BQP).
    *   *Complexity structure:* Complete.

### 5. The dividing line
The pattern across all three structures is:
**Complexity is active when:**
*   The physical observable is the minimal gate count or circuit depth to prepare a state.
*   The geometric property of a bulk space is identified with boundary computational cost.
*   The physical phenomenon is protected by or mapped to a computational complexity class (QMA, BQP).

**Complexity is a language when:**
*   The system is just "hard to solve analytically" (like the classical 3-body problem), but not computationally intractable in the complexity-theoretic sense.
*   The "complexity" is just a synonym for "large entanglement entropy" (which is a statistical property, not a computational one).

**Complexity is inapplicable when:**
*   The system is efficiently simulable classically (free fermions, 1D area-law states).
*   The physics is purely thermodynamic (where entropy and temperature suffice, and fine-grained computational cost is irrelevant).
*   The dynamics are continuous and classical (where discrete gate counts have no meaning).

### 6. The one-sentence summary
Complexity theory shines in physics at the level of circuit geometry, holographic duality, and computational hardness when the structures involved possess non-trivial gate-count scaling, bulk-boundary computational dictionaries, and intractability classes — as in black hole interiors, quantum supremacy, and topological error correction — and it is inapplicable when the structures are efficiently simulable, purely thermodynamic, or classical.

### 1. Complexity does not generate dynamics

## The organizing idea

Complexity does not shine in physics as the observation that nature has many parts, as the slogan that everything is a computer, as the Kolmogorov complexity of a description, or as P-versus-NP enthusiasm applied to cells and markets. Many-partedness is not complexity. A slogan is not a scaling claim. Complexity shines in **three specific mathematical structures**: **hardness** (the cost of prediction), **scrambling** (the spread of information), and **growth** (the depth of history). Not all computation, not every resource count — a specific, identifiable subclass.

The dividing line is this: **complexity is present when the cost of computation is a physical fact — when difficulty is a property of the system rather than a confession of the observer.** Without that, complexity is our inconvenience. With it, complexity prices what nature hides, tracks what nature mixes, and clocks what nature accumulates.

**One history, three ledgers: what it costs us to track it (hardness), how fast the system mixes it away (scrambling), and how deep it piles up (growth).**

A position note. The six previous maps described structures *of* the world: the constitution at the corners, the substance in the bulk, the grammar throughout, the motion itself, the seal on the unchangeable, the evidence in the answer. Complexity is the first structure that belongs to neither the world nor its mathematics alone — it is the cost of *getting from one to the other*. The charter is Feynman (1981–82), with Cook–Levin (1971) as the ancestor: computation itself has intractable problems, and physics has them too. And the series has been collecting this map's pieces for a while — calculus conceded that chaos parts uniqueness from predictability; spectra flagged the gap as undecidable; geometry left holography open; algebra confessed it never chooses the entanglement's factorization; topology and complexity will find they share a code. This map collects the wall — and its founding discovery is that the wall is not ours. The observer's costs turn out to be the system's own.

## 1. Hardness (the cost of prediction)

### What it is here

The classification of physical prediction by its asymptotic cost. The basic unit is the polynomial/exponential divide — polynomial scaling stays in reach as $N$ grows; exponential leaves any physical computer. Honest at the foundation: the divide rests on the unproven $\mathrm{P} \ne \mathrm{NP}$ and on the extended Church–Turing thesis — the only map in the series whose basic unit is an unproven axiom. And honest at the seam: the divide is asymptotic, physics works at finite $N$, and that seam is exactly where the audit wars live (§8).

### Four levels of hardness content

**Level 1 — The divide itself.** Polynomial versus exponential as the criterion of tractability. Not a theorem of nature — a constitution for our claims about nature, and the load-bearing assumption underneath every quantum-advantage statement.

**Level 2 — Hardness in the wild.** The Ising spin glass in a field is NP-hard (Barahona 1982); exact partition functions are #P-hard (Valiant). No efficient general algorithm exists unless complexity's conjectures fail. The physical shadow: glassy dynamics — aging, memory, ultra-slow relaxation — as the dynamical face of a computationally intractable landscape.

**Level 3 — Undecidability.** The spectral gap for families of 2D lattice Hamiltonians is undecidable (Cubitt–Pérez-García–Wolf 2015): reducible from the halting problem through aperiodic tilings, with gap status that can be independent of the axioms of mathematics. This is the far wall — not expensive, but impossible.

**Level 4 — The quantum boundary.** Feynman's founding argument: generic quantum dynamics cannot be tracked classically at polynomial cost. Made precise by sampling complexity — exact sampling from circuit outputs is #P-hard, and approximate sampling would collapse the polynomial hierarchy under stated assumptions (Stockmeyer; Aaronson–Arkhipov) — and demonstrated by random-circuit and boson-sampling experiments, with classical tensor-network spoofing continuously moving the bar.

### The identification

| Physical structure | Complexity object | Identification |
|---|---|---|
| Spin glass ground state | NP-hard decision problem | Landscape hardness |
| Exact partition function | #P counting | The cost of the sum |
| Lattice spectral gap | Undecidable predicate | The wall beyond hardness |
| Factoring | In BQP; no classical polynomial route known | The wall's conditional breach |
| Random circuit output | Hard-to-sample distribution | The supremacy claim |

### Where complexity shines

**Strongest case: the founding inversion — the wall becomes the machine.** The negative theorem (classical simulation of generic quantum dynamics is exponentially costly) was inverted into a technology: quantum computing is a complexity-theoretic *prediction about physics*, and Shor's algorithm is its sharpest point — arithmetic's hardest famous problem, solved efficiently on a device, because what is hard is a fact about the physical world, not about mathematics alone. Honest notes throughout: BQP sits inside PSPACE (the escape is from the classical wall, not from mathematics'); the largest quantum-factored integer is 21; the demos are finite and audited. The claim that survives every audit: the exponential wall is real, and nature is on the other side of it.

**Second: the thermodynamics of information.** Landauer's principle — erasing a bit costs $k_B T \ln 2$ (Landauer 1961), *measured* (Bérut et al. 2012 and successors) — plus Bennett's reversible computing (1973, 1982), which exorcises Maxwell's demon: the demon's memory is physical, its bookkeeping has a price, and the price has been paid in the laboratory. The first measurement of information's materiality.

**Third: glassy hardness.** Barahona's theorem as a physical fact about spin-glass landscapes: aging and memory effects as the shadow of intractability, and the reason "just find the ground state" is not a physics program. Honest scope: hardness of the general problem is not hardness of every instance.

**Fourth: the undecidable gap.** The spectra map's ⚠ becomes native here: the master dichotomy of matter — gapped or not — is not merely uncomputed but uncomputable in general. The sharpest known edge in the series' catalogue of dividing lines.

### Where complexity is not active

- **Low-entanglement regimes.** DMRG and tensor networks (White 1992 onward) simulate enormous systems efficiently — the wall is conditional on entanglement growth, and physics is full of nongeneric exceptions.
- **Integrable models.** Bethe ansatz routes around the wall entirely; solvability is complexity's exemption.
- **Energy barriers.** Arrhenius hardness is quantitative; exponential cost is a different species. **A stiff wall is not a computational wall.**

## 2. Scrambling (the spread of information)

### What it is here

The dynamics of quantum information: how locally injected information spreads, hides, and equilibrates. Classical chaos had Lyapunov exponents; quantum chaos for decades had only level statistics (the spectra map's fingerprint) — until diagnostics for the *dynamics* of information were built.

### Four levels of scrambling content

**Level 1 — Lieb–Robinson bounds (1972).** An effective light cone for nonrelativistic lattice dynamics: correlations are exponentially suppressed outside a linear cone. Relativity's structure emerging from quantum mechanics plus local Hamiltonians — no photons required. The calculus map's hyperbolicity, re-derived informationally.

**Level 2 — OTOCs and the butterfly effect.** The out-of-time-order correlator, born with Larkin–Ovchinnikov (1969) and resurrected in the modern era: $C(t) = \langle [W(t),V(0)]^\dagger [W(t),V(0)]\rangle$, the commutator squared as the chaos meter, with the exponential growth rate bounded by the chaos bound $\lambda_L \le 2\pi k_B T/\hbar$ (Maldacena–Shenker–Stanford 2016) and a shape — the butterfly velocity. Measured in trapped ions, superconducting qubits, NMR.

**Level 3 — Fast scramblers and black holes.** The scrambling time $t_* \sim \frac{\beta}{2\pi}\log S$: logarithmic in entropy, with black holes saturating the bound — the fastest scramblers in nature (Sekino–Susskind 2008). Hayden–Preskill (2007): information thrown in is re-emitted in the Hawking radiation after $t_*$ — the black hole as nature's fastest mirror. Shenker–Stanford shockwaves: the chaos diagnostics acquiring *geometry* — the butterfly effect has a metric.

**Level 4 — Thermalization and its failure.** ETH: generic chaotic eigenstates are fully scrambled, local observables see thermal values, and the Wigner–Dyson spacings underwrite it. MBL: the anti-scrambling phase — local integrals of motion, Poisson statistics, information that never spreads (Basko–Aleiner–Altshuler 2006; Nandkishore–Huse 2015). And the newest instruments: operator growth and Krylov complexity, tracking the operator's spread through its own Lanczos space.

### The identification

| Physical structure | Complexity object | Identification |
|---|---|---|
| Quasiparticle front | Lieb–Robinson cone | Speed limit without relativity |
| Quantum chaos | OTOC growth; $\lambda_L$ | The butterfly effect, metered |
| Black hole | $t_* \sim \frac{\beta}{2\pi}\log S$ | Fast-scrambler bound, saturated |
| Hawking decoding | Hayden–Preskill time | The fastest mirror |
| MBL phase | Poisson spacings + LIOMs | The anti-scrambler |

### Where complexity shines

**Strongest case: the fast-scrambler bound and its saturation.** A universal bound on how fast information can spread, *proved* (MSS 2016), and *attained* by black holes — nature's limit reached by nature's most generic object. With Hayden–Preskill as the most counterintuitive consequence in the series: under ideal conditions, throwing your diary into a black hole makes it *more* recoverable. Status honest: holographic theorems; the OTOC meters themselves are laboratory-real at small scale.

**Second: the OTOC as chaos's quantum meter.** Level statistics are static; the OTOC is dynamic. The two diagnostics pair across the maps — spacings (spectra's word) and butterfly velocities (this map's word) are two readings of one chaos, and the OTOC has been measured on chip.

**Third: Lieb–Robinson.** The foundation of rigorous many-body physics: it bounds simulation cost, underlies area laws, and hands nonrelativistic quantum mechanics an emergent causal structure. Measured as quasiparticle light cones in cold atoms. The hedge: the cone is proven linear; its slope is not a universal constant.

**Fourth: MBL as the information vault.** The phase where scrambling fails and local memory persists — the spectra map's Poisson diagnosis with this map's mechanism. Honest: whether ideal thermodynamic MBL exists has been seriously questioned (avoided transitions); the vault may leak at the far limit.

### Where complexity is not active

- **Integrable systems.** Generalized Gibbs, no chaos — the OTOC never grows exponentially; no scrambling to diagnose.
- **Free systems.** Ballistic spreading without mixing — *mixing is not chaos*, and the butterfly needs the exponential.
- **Small systems.** The scrambling time is a thermodynamic-limit quantity; two qubits have no log $S$.

## 3. Growth (the depth of history)

### What it is here

Complexity as a property of states and unitaries with dynamics of its own — the candidate second entropy. Entropy grows until equilibrium and saturates. Complexity does not: it is the clock that keeps running.

### Four levels of growth content

**Level 1 — Circuit complexity of states.** The minimum number of gates to prepare a state from a reference. A counting argument (sharpened by Hastings) shows almost all states in Hilbert space require exponentially many gates — so *physical states*, reached by time evolution from low-complexity references, are a measure-zero, complexity-bounded subset. The universe lives on the easy manifold; why is deferred to §8. Honest note: complexity is defined relative to a gate set — but natural universal gate sets agree up to sub-polynomial corrections, making the measure nearly intrinsic.

**Level 2 — The second law of complexity.** Complexity grows *linearly* in boundary time for a time exponential in the entropy ($t \sim e^S$) before saturating — long after every thermodynamic observable has gone quiet. Susskind's proposal: complexity is the second entropy, the clock of the deep interior, the dynamical variable that keeps moving when the thermometer stops.

**Level 3 — Complexity as geometry.** Nielsen's geometric approach: circuit complexity as geodesic length in the manifold of unitaries — the geometry map's geodesy, rediscovered inside computation. The CV and CA conjectures (Susskind and collaborators): the complexity of the boundary state equals the volume — or the action — of the bulk interior behind the horizon, whose growth long after exterior stationarity *is* complexity growth. And the practical half: tensor networks — DMRG, MERA (Swingle 2012) — as circuits whose shape is a spacetime. The algebra map's boundary case — entanglement described perfectly, factorization never chosen — becomes here an economy: tensor networks are the discipline of *choosing* factorizations, and their cost is the subject.

**Level 4 — The boundary of the easy, and the phase of the reliable.** Gottesman–Knill: the stabilizer fragment of quantum mechanics is exactly and efficiently simulable — the easiest subtheory is precisely an algebra, and quantum advantage begins where the algebra ends (magic states as the fuel). The threshold theorem (Aharonov–Ben-Or; Kitaev; Knill–Laflamme–Zurek): below an error threshold, arbitrarily long reliable computation — a phase transition in the space of channels, now demonstrated below threshold in surface-code hardware (2023). Self-correction — matter that remembers by its own thermodynamics — in sight (Haah's cubic code's logarithmic barriers) and not in hand; fractons as the newest shared province with topology: excitations whose mobility the code structure forbids.

### The identification

| Physical structure | Complexity object | Identification |
|---|---|---|
| Black hole interior | CV: volume ∝ boundary time | Complexity growth = interior growth |
| Late-time dynamics | Second law of complexity | The clock after entropy |
| Typical states | Exponential circuit complexity | The unreachable bulk of Hilbert space |
| Stabilizer fragment | Gottesman–Knill | The exactly simulable algebra |
| Below-threshold memory | Threshold theorem | Computation as a phase |

### Where complexity shines

**Strongest case: tensor networks and the complexity–geometry complex.** Practically: DMRG and its descendants are the working machinery of modern condensed matter — the most-used computational tool any map in this series has claimed, daily confirmed. Conceptually: the proposal that geometry is the circuit's shape. The strongest *confirmed* case is the tool; the strongest *proposed* case is the identification (§5).

**Second: the second law of complexity.** A genuine new-law candidate: entropy saturates, complexity climbs; the black hole interior as complexity's ledger. Within holography, robust; as universal physics, proposed.

**Third: Gottesman–Knill as the exact boundary of the easy.** The one place in physics where "which algebra you are in" *provably* decides the computational cost — a theorem linking the grammar to the ledger with no conjecture in between.

**Fourth: the threshold theorem and error-corrected hardware.** Computation as a phase of matter, with a transition that has now been crossed in the laboratory; decoding as a physical process running at clock speed.

### Where complexity is not active

- **Early times.** Until entropy saturates, complexity is invisible — entropy suffices, and the map's whole domain is the deep interior of dynamics.
- **Any single small experiment.** Complexity is asymptotic and unmeasured directly; only proxies exist (OTOCs, form factors, cross-entropy). There is no complexity-meter.
- **The reference.** Gate set, reference state, cost model: inputs. Complexity measures distance from choices it does not make.

- The scaling of computational cost is a physical fact — what can be simulated, sampled, or decoded.
- Information spreading has its own limits and meters (Lieb–Robinson cones, OTOCs, scrambling times).
- Complexity is a state property with dynamics of its own (the second law, CV).
- The device or material is organized by computational concerns (thresholds, codes).

**Complexity is a language when:**

- "Complex" means merely many-parted or intricate — complication, not depth.
- Resource counts are quoted without asymptotic stakes or physical stakes.
- Complexity classes are labels hung on phenomena that do not depend on them.

**Complexity is silent when:**

- The question is *which problem* — the Hamiltonian, the input, the oracle: input, every time. Complexity classifies the cost of what is posed; it never poses anything.
- The question is probability: the Born rule — the wall all seven maps share. Complexity's nearest approach is sideways: Landauer prices the *bookkeeping* of measurement, and typicality measures states — but the chance of outcomes is unclaimed, as in all six maps before.
- The question is the *actual*. Complexity speaks of generic states; the universe occupies a special, low-complexity history. Genericity is the tool; actuality is not its output.
- The question is the dynamics itself: complexity meters the flow, and does not run it. The engine is calculus's.

The dividing line is **whether the cost of computation is a physical fact.** If the difficulty of predicting is a property of the system, complexity is physics. If it is a property of our position, complexity is psychology with a thesaurus.

Three distinctions carry the weight:

- **Complicated ≠ complex** (many parts is not depth of history; size is not cost).
- **Random ≠ complex** (randomness is easy to generate and hard to simulate — the supremacy subtlety: random circuits are powerful *because* they are structureless, and unverifiable *for the same reason*).
- **Hard ≠ uncomputable** (complexity is about scaling; the true wall is undecidability, met rarely — at the gap).

## 8. Capacities and limits

### ✅ Five capacities

1. **The wall and the escape** — Feynman's founding argument through Shor to the supremacy experiments; Harlow–Hayden's complexity wall guarding complementarity. Strongest: the only negative theorem in the series that became an industry.
2. **Information's thermodynamics** — Landauer's $k_B T \ln 2$, measured; the demon exorcised by Bennett; information's materiality on the laboratory record.
3. **Scrambling diagnostics** — OTOCs and butterfly velocities measured; Lieb–Robinson cones proved and observed; the fast-scrambler bound proved and saturated by black holes.
4. **The second clock** — the second law of complexity; late-time dynamics invisible to entropy; CV/CA as the holographic ledger.
5. **Computation as a phase** — the threshold theorem, crossed in hardware; codes as phases of matter; decoding as real-time physics.

### ❌ Four silences

1. **Complexity does not select its problems.** Hamiltonian, input distribution, oracle, gate set: inputs, every one — the series' recurring silence, in its most formal dress.
2. **Complexity does not ground probability.** The Born rule stands outside all seven maps. Complexity prices the erasure, measures the ensemble, and leaves the chance exactly where it began.
3. **Complexity does not explain the actual.** Almost all states are exponential-complexity mush; the physical world is a low-complexity exception. Why the easy manifold is occupied is the standing puzzle this map inherits and cannot close.
4. **Complexity does not run the dynamics.** The unitary is input; scrambling is a property of the flow, complexity growth a property of the flow's wake. Complexity is the speedometer on calculus's engine — accurate, and not once in the driver's seat.

### ⚠️ Three boundary cases

1. **Supremacy and the audit wars.** The demos are finite, the claims asymptotic, and hyper-optimized tensor-network contractions repeatedly lower the bar; the boundary between quantum and classical moves with every classical algorithm. Status: PARTIALLY APPLICABLE.
2. **The complexity arrow.** Entropy saturates; complexity climbs. Whether the late arrow of time *is* complexity growth — or merely presupposes the thermodynamic arrow it extends — is open and unmeasured. Status: PARTIALLY APPLICABLE.
3. **Complexity = geometry (CV/CA).** Theorems in toy models, tension between the volume and action versions, no handle on any interior. Status: PARTIALLY APPLICABLE — the sharpest open question in the series, shared with the geometry map's frontier.

## 10. The one-sentence summary

**Complexity shines in physics at the level of hardness, scrambling, and growth — when the cost of prediction is a physical fact, as in the exponential wall that Feynman turned into quantum computing and Harlow–Hayden turned into the resolution of the firewall paradox; when information-spreading has its own limits, as in Lieb–Robinson light cones, OTOC butterfly velocities, and the fast-scrambler bound that black holes saturate; and when complexity itself is a quantity, as in the second law of complexity and the identification of the black hole interior's growth with boundary circuit complexity — and it is a language rather than physics when "complex" merely means intricate, and it is silent when the question is which problem, what probability, which actual state, or what drives the flow it only meters.**

And the one-paragraph version, closing the septet:

> **Complexity in physics is the ledger — the first structure in the series that belongs to the observer, and the discovery that the observer's costs are the system's own. It shines brightest when physics demands a price: what it costs to predict (exponential, generically), to decode (Harlow–Hayden's wall, guarding complementarity by intractability), to erase (Landauer's $k_B T\ln 2$, measured), to scramble (the log-S bound, proved and saturated by black holes), to keep computing (the threshold theorem, crossed in hardware). It is silent when physics asks which problem is posed, which probability attaches, which low-complexity history the universe actually took — inputs and postulates, every one — and on the Born rule it does not even approach: it prices the bookkeeping of measurement and leaves the chance unclaimed, as all seven maps have. Its frontier is the series' boldest identification — that the interior of spacetime is the boundary's computation, entanglement for the bulk's extent, complexity for its duration, it from qubit completed as it from complexity — status partial, theorems in toy models, no handle on the interior. The seven maps now close: arithmetic is the constitution at the sharp corners, geometry the substance in the bulk, algebra the grammar throughout, calculus the motion itself, topology the seal on what cannot change, spectra the evidence of it all, complexity the price of it all — and they meet, as always, at the index theorem and the modular tensor category, and now at the black hole interior and the error-correcting code, where cost joins curvature, count, class, and spectrum. Arithmetic counts; geometry shapes; algebra acts; calculus flows; topology endures; spectra reveals; complexity deepens. And the Born rule stands outside all seven — the one postulate no census, no stage, no grammar, no engine, no seal, no evidence, no ledger has ever claimed.**
