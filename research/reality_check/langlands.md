## 1. Dualities

### What a duality is

A duality is an equivalence between two seemingly different physical theories. It maps the states, operators, and observables of one theory to those of the other. In physics, dualities include S-duality, T-duality, mirror symmetry, and holography.

### When does a duality have Langlands content?

A generic duality relates two theories with the same degrees of freedom and the same symmetries. Most dualities have no arithmetic structure beyond their group theory.

A duality has Langlands content when the mapping involves a **replacement of the gauge group by its Langlands dual** and an **inversion of the coupling constant**.

**Four levels of Langlands content in dualities:**

**Level 1 — Abelian electric-magnetic duality.** Maxwell's equations are invariant under the exchange of electric and magnetic fields. This is the simplest case, with gauge group U(1).

**Level 2 — Non-abelian electric-magnetic duality.** Montonen-Olive S-duality relates a gauge theory with gauge group \(G\) and coupling \(g\) to a gauge theory with the Langlands dual group \(^L G\) and coupling \(1/g\).

**Level 3 — Geometric Langlands correspondence.** The S-duality of N=4 super Yang-Mills compactified on a Riemann surface is realized as the geometric Langlands correspondence, a duality between categories of D-modules and coherent sheaves.

**Level 4 — Arithmetic Langlands correspondence.** The original Langlands program relates Galois representations to automorphic forms, which appear in the arithmetic of elliptic curves and modular forms.

### The duality

The duality in this case is between the **physical gauge theory** and a **number-theoretic object**.

| Physical duality | Langlands object | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| S-duality of N=4 SYM | Geometric Langlands correspondence | Topological field theory |
| Electric-magnetic duality | Langlands dual group \(^L G\) | Root data |
| Holographic duality | Arithmetic Langlands program | Automorphic forms |
| Mirror symmetry | Langlands correspondence | Moduli spaces |

### Where Langlands shines

**Strongest case: the geometric Langlands correspondence.** Kapustin and Witten showed that the geometric Langlands correspondence arises naturally as an aspect of Montonen-Olive S-duality. The S-duality of N=4 super Yang-Mills on a four-manifold, when compactified on a Riemann surface and topologically twisted, yields exactly the categories of D-modules and coherent sheaves appearing in the geometric Langlands program. This is not an analogy; it is a physical derivation of a deep mathematical correspondence.

**Second strongest: arithmetic Langlands in string theory.** The arithmetic Langlands program has been applied to two problems in string physics: deriving the geometry of spacetime from worldsheet dynamics (emergent spacetime) and using automorphic black holes as probes of extra dimensions.

**Third: mirror symmetry and the Langlands program.** The geometric Langlands duality is physically understandable via mirror symmetries between electric and magnetic branes associated with the boundaries of the manifold.

**Speculative: Langlands and quantum gravity.** The six-dimensional origin of the Kapustin-Witten construction suggests possible connections to quantum gravity, but these remain speculative.

### Where Langlands is not active

- Dualities that do not involve a coupling constant inversion.
- Dualities in non-supersymmetric, non-topological theories.

## 2. Invariants

### What an invariant is

An invariant is a quantity preserved under a specified class of transformations. In physics, invariants include conserved charges, topological numbers, and scaling exponents.

### When does an invariant have Langlands content?

A generic invariant is a real number. It can be continuous. It can vary under small perturbations. It has no arithmetic structure.

An invariant has Langlands content when it takes values in a **discrete set with number-theoretic structure**: Galois eigenvalues, Hecke eigenvalues, or values of an L-function.

**Four types of Langlands invariants:**

**Type 1 — Galois eigenvalues.** The Galois group acts on spaces of BPS states in certain 1+1 dimensional theories with (2,2) supersymmetry. The central charges are valued in a Galois extension of ℚ, and the Galois group acts on the space of BPS states.

**Type 2 — Hecke eigenvalues.** In the geometric Langlands program, Hecke operators act on the moduli space of bundles, and their eigenvalues are the arithmetic invariants that classify the corresponding automorphic forms.

**Type 3 — Automorphic forms.** The partition function of certain string compactifications is an automorphic form, and its Fourier coefficients encode the physical spectrum.

**Type 4 — L-function values.** Special values of L-functions are conjecturally related to physical observables. The Freund-Witten p-adic amplitude is the closest case.

### The duality

The duality is between the **physical invariant** and a **number-theoretic quantity**.

| Physical invariant | Langlands quantity | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| BPS state central charge | Galois representation | Arithmetic |
| Moduli space coordinate | Hecke eigenvalue | Automorphic |
| String partition function | Automorphic form | Modular |
| Black hole entropy | Siegel modular form | Arithmetic |

### Where Langlands shines

**Strongest case: Hecke operators in arithmetic quantum chaos.** The Hecke operators generate an arithmetic symmetry of the spectrum of the Laplacian on arithmetic surfaces. They commute with the Laplacian and have eigenvalues that are algebraic. This is the Langlands program in its spectral incarnation.

**Second strongest: BPS states and Galois representations.** The BPS states of certain 1+1 dimensional theories with (2,2) supersymmetry naturally carry an arithmetic structure: the central charges are valued in a Galois extension of ℚ, and conjecturally, the Galois group acts on the spaces of BPS states.

**Third: automorphic black holes.** The entropy of certain black holes in string theory is computed via the asymptotic growth of Fourier coefficients of modular forms, which are arithmetic objects.

**Fourth: emergent spacetime from modular forms.** The geometry of spacetime is conjecturally derived from the worldsheet dynamics of string theory, which leads to modular forms that encode arithmetic data.

### Where Langlands is not active

- Generic topological invariants (Chern numbers) are integers, but the integers come from topology, not arithmetic.
- Energy conservation is real-valued, not arithmetic.
- Momentum conservation is real-valued, not arithmetic.

The distinction is crucial: **integer-valued ≠ arithmetic**. Integers appear everywhere in physics, but Langlands content requires Galois or automorphic structure beyond integer-valuedness.

## 3. Symmetries

### What a symmetry is

A symmetry is a transformation that leaves a physical structure invariant. Symmetries form groups, and the representations of these groups organize physical states.

### When does a symmetry have Langlands content?

A generic symmetry is a Lie group (continuous) or a discrete group (finite or infinite). Most symmetries have no arithmetic content beyond their group structure.

A symmetry has Langlands content when the group is **arithmetic** — that is, when it is defined by number-theoretic conditions, or when the symmetry is a **duality group** that exchanges a group with its Langlands dual.

**Four types of Langlands symmetry:**

**Type 1 — Discrete Lie group.** A finite subgroup of a Lie group. No arithmetic content.

**Type 2 — Lattice symmetry.** A discrete subgroup of a Lie group with a lattice structure. No arithmetic content.

**Type 3 — Arithmetic group.** A subgroup of GL(n, ℚ) defined by arithmetic conditions. This is the first level of Langlands content.

**Type 4 — Langlands duality symmetry.** A symmetry that exchanges a gauge group \(G\) with its Langlands dual \(^L G\). This is the deepest level of Langlands content.

### The duality

The duality is between the **physical symmetry** and a **number-theoretic object**.

| Physical symmetry | Langlands object | Duality |
|---|---|---|
| S-duality of N=4 SYM | Langlands dual group | Coupling inversion |
| Hecke operators | Arithmetic correspondences | Generating structure |
| Galois group | Algebraic number field | Automorphism action |
| Modular group SL(2, ℤ) | Modular forms | Action on upper half-plane |

### Where Langlands shines

**Strongest case: S-duality as the Langlands dual group.** The Montonen-Olive S-duality states that a gauge theory with gauge group \(G\) and coupling \(g\) is equivalent to a gauge theory with the Langlands dual group \(^L G\) and coupling \(1/g\). This is a physical symmetry with Langlands structure.

**Second strongest: geometric Langlands from S-duality.** Kapustin and Witten showed that the geometric Langlands correspondence arises from S-duality of N=4 super Yang-Mills compactified on a Riemann surface. S-duality swaps the group \(G\) with its Langlands dual \(^L G\) and acts antipodally on the family of twists.

**Third: Hecke operators in arithmetic quantum chaos.** The Hecke operators generate an arithmetic symmetry of the spectrum of the Laplacian on arithmetic surfaces. They commute with the Laplacian and have eigenvalues that are algebraic.

**Fourth: Galois symmetry in rational CFT.** The Galois group of the cyclotomic field acts on the set of primary fields. This is a deep arithmetic symmetry of rational CFTs.

### Where Langlands is not active

- Poincaré symmetry (spacetime translations and Lorentz transformations): Lie group, no Langlands content.
- Gauge symmetry SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1): compact Lie group, no Langlands content.
- Diffeomorphism group of GR: infinite-dimensional Lie group, no Langlands content.

The distinction: **discrete ≠ arithmetic**. Discrete symmetries can be finite groups or infinite groups, and most have no Langlands content beyond their group structure.

# Where the Langlands Program Shines in Physics: Dualities, Spectra, and Cross-Description Translations

## The organizing idea
The Langlands program does not shine in physics as a generator of continuous motion, a measure of spatial distance, or a calculator of statistical probabilities. It shines in three specific mathematical structures: **geometric S-duality (the Kapustin-Witten bridge), integrable systems and spectral curves (Hitchin fibrations and opers), and modular/arithmetic invariants (partition functions and topological phases)**. Not all gauge theories, not all quantum systems, not all symmetries — but a specific, identifiable subclass where the "Rosetta Stone" of mathematics perfectly translates between distinct physical descriptions.

The dividing line is this: the Langlands program is present when a physical equivalence maps the *representation theory of a gauge group* $G$ to the *geometric or arithmetic data of a dual structure* (the Langlands dual group $^LG$, a spectral curve, or a modular space). Without this cross-description translation between algebraic representations and geometric/arithmetic data, Langlands is absent. With it, Langlands provides the ultimate dictionary for the deepest dualities in theoretical physics.

Below: the three structures, the duality in each, and the class of physical systems where the Langlands program is genuinely active.

### 1. Geometric S-Duality and Gauge Theory (The Kapustin-Witten Bridge)
**What it is**
The physical realization of the Geometric Langlands correspondence. In 2006, Kapustin and Witten demonstrated that the S-duality (electric-magnetic duality) of 4D $\mathcal{N}=4$ Super Yang-Mills theory is exactly the Geometric Langlands program. 

**When does a structure have Langlands content?**
A generic duality just maps one set of variables to another. A structure has Langlands content when the duality exchanges the representation theory of a gauge group $G$ (Wilson loops, electric charges) with the geometric/arithmetic data of the Langlands dual group $^LG$ ('t Hooft loops, magnetic charges, D-modules).

**Four levels of Langlands content in gauge theory:**
*   **Level 1 — Classical S-Duality.** The exchange of electric and magnetic charges. This appears in: Montonen-Olive duality, $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM.
*   **Level 2 — Geometric Langlands.** The equivalence between $G$-bundles and $^LG$-local systems on a Riemann surface. This appears in: Kapustin-Witten twist of $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM.
*   **Level 3 — Quantum Geometric Langlands.** The deformation of the correspondence by quantum parameters ($q$-deformation). This appears in: 5D gauge theories compactified on a circle, quantum groups.
*   **Level 4 — Arithmetic Langlands.** The number-theoretic limit where the Riemann surface is replaced by an arithmetic curve over a finite field. This appears in: p-adic string theory, adelic physics.

**The duality**
The duality is between the gauge theory and the geometric/arithmetic dual.
| Physical concept (Group $G$) | Langlands object (Dual $^LG$) | Duality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wilson loop (Electric) | 't Hooft loop (Magnetic) | S-duality / Langlands duality |
| Gauge bundle / Connection | Local system / Flat connection | Geometric Langlands |
| Brane boundary conditions | D-modules / Hecke eigensheaves | Kapustin-Witten correspondence |
| Coupling constant $\tau$ | Modular parameter $-1/\tau$ | Electric-magnetic inversion |

**Where Langlands shines**
*   **Strongest case: Proving S-Duality.** The Kapustin-Witten construction provides the most rigorous mathematical framework for understanding electric-magnetic duality in 4D. It translates a physical conjecture into a proven mathematical theorem (Geometric Langlands).
*   **Second strongest: Surface Operators and Defects.** The behavior of line and surface defects in gauge theories is perfectly classified by the Langlands correspondence, mapping physical defects to geometric objects (Hecke modifications).
*   **Third: Mirror Symmetry.** The geometric Langlands correspondence is deeply intertwined with mirror symmetry for certain moduli spaces (like the Hitchin moduli space), linking symplectic and complex geometry.

**Where Langlands is not active**
*   Non-supersymmetric QCD (where confinement and mass gaps break the exact S-duality required for the Langlands correspondence).
*   Purely classical mechanics (which lacks the gauge-theoretic and quantum structures required for the duality).
*   Theories without a clear geometric or arithmetic dual space.

### 2. Integrable Systems and Spectral Curves (The Hitchin Fibration)
**What it is**
The application of Langlands-type correspondences to quantum integrable systems, where the spectrum of a quantum Hamiltonian is determined by the geometry of a classical spectral curve (the Hitchin system) and the theory of opers.

**When does a structure have integrable Langlands content?**
A generic quantum system has a messy spectrum. A structure has integrable Langlands content when the quantum states (e.g., Bethe Ansatz roots) are in exact bijection with geometric objects (opers) on a spectral curve, and the commuting Hamiltonians are generated by Hecke operators or Hitchin Hamiltonians.

**Four types of integrable Langlands structures:**
*   **Type 1 — Gaudin Models.** Quantum spin chains whose spectrum is governed by opers for the Langlands dual group. This appears in: The geometric Langlands correspondence for the Gaudin system (Feigin-Frenkel).
*   **Type 2 — Hitchin Systems.** Classical integrable systems on the moduli space of bundles. This appears in: The spectral curve of the Hitchin system dictating the quantum spectrum.
*   **Type 3 — Quantum Spectral Curves.** The quantization of the classical spectral curve, where the wavefunction satisfies a differential equation (an oper). This appears in: Quantum KdV, quantum Seiberg-Witten theory.
*   **Type 4 — Affine Gaudin / Cyclotomic Models.** Integrable systems with irregular singularities, linking to arithmetic data and isomonodromic deformations.

**The duality**
The duality is between the quantum spectrum and the geometric oper.
| Physical concept | Langlands/Geometric object | Duality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bethe Ansatz roots | Monodromy / Opers | Spectral duality |
| Commuting Hamiltonians | Hitchin Hamiltonians | Integrability |
| Quantum wavefunction | D-module / Hecke eigensheaf | Quantum Langlands |
| Seiberg-Witten curve | Spectral curve of the Hitchin system | 4D/2D correspondence |

**Where Langlands shines**
*   **Strongest case: Exact Solvability of Spin Chains.** The Feigin-Frenkel correspondence proves that the spectrum of the Gaudin spin chain is exactly the space of opers for the Langlands dual group. This is Langlands as the ultimate tool for exact quantum solvability.
*   **Second strongest: Quantum Seiberg-Witten Theory.** The low-energy effective action of 4D $\mathcal{N}=2$ gauge theories is governed by a spectral curve that is mathematically identical to the Hitchin spectral curve, linking 4D physics to 2D integrable systems via Langlands.
*   **Third: 2D Conformal Field Theory.** The conformal blocks of rational CFTs are governed by the representation theory of affine Lie algebras, which is deeply controlled by the geometric Langlands correspondence at the critical level.

**Where Langlands is not active**
*   Non-integrable, chaotic quantum systems (where no spectral curve or oper exists to organize the spectrum).
*   Systems without a large number of conserved charges (where the Hitchin fibration degenerates).

### 3. Modular Invariants and Arithmetic Echoes (The Global Accountant)
**What it is**
The appearance of automorphic forms, modular forms, and Hecke operators in the partition functions, topological invariants, and arithmetic limits of physical theories. This is the "arithmetic" wing of the Langlands program manifesting in physics.

**When does a structure have modular/arithmetic Langlands content?**
A generic partition function is just a sum. A structure has modular Langlands content when the partition function transforms as an automorphic form under a discrete group (like $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$), or when physical observables are governed by Hecke operators and L-functions.

**Four types of modular/arithmetic Langlands structures:**
*   **Type 1 — Rational CFT and Modular Tensor Categories.** The partition function is a modular form; the fusion rules are governed by the modular S-matrix. This appears in: 2D CFTs, WZW models.
*   **Type 2 — Topological Phases and Anyons.** The braiding and fusion of anyons are described by modular tensor categories, whose data (S and T matrices) satisfy arithmetic relations akin to Galois actions. This appears in: Fractional Quantum Hall Effect, topological quantum computation.
*   **Type 3 — Black Hole Microstates.** The asymptotic growth of BPS states is governed by the Fourier coefficients of mock modular forms. This appears in: String theory, supersymmetric black holes.
*   **Type 4 — Adelic and p-adic Physics.** The physical amplitude is an adelic product over all primes, linking continuous and discrete physics via the Langlands-style product formula. This appears in: Freund-Witten p-adic strings, Bost-Connes system.

**The duality**
The duality is between the physical observable and the automorphic/arithmetic object.
| Physical concept | Langlands/Arithmetic object | Duality |
| --- | --- | --- |

**Where Langlands shines**
*   **Strongest case: Classifying Topological Order.** The modular S and T matrices of a topological phase are the exact physical realization of the modular data in the Langlands program. They strictly classify which topological phases can exist.
*   **Second strongest: Black Hole Entropy.** The exact microscopic counting of black hole states relies on the Hardy-Ramanujan-Rademacher circle method, applying the modular properties of the partition function (a core Langlands/arithmetic tool) to extract the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.
*   **Third: Cross-Place Consistency.** The Freund-Witten adelic product formula shows that the physical string amplitude is globally consistent across all "places" (real and p-adic), a direct physical manifestation of the arithmetic Langlands philosophy.

**Where Langlands is not active**
*   Generic thermal systems (where the partition function does not possess modular or automorphic symmetry).
*   Non-topological phases of matter (where the data is continuous and not protected by modular arithmetic).
*   Perturbative quantum field theory (where amplitudes are calculated via Feynman diagrams, not automorphic forms).

### 5. The dividing line

**Langlands is active when:**

# Where the Langlands Program Shines in Physics: Duality, Automorphy, Correspondence

## The organizing idea

The Langlands program does not shine in physics as a theory of everything, as an explanation of particle masses or generations, as the fact that modular forms appear in partition functions (that finding belongs to the arithmetic map), or as the promise that the Riemann hypothesis will fall to a Hamiltonian. A visited conjecture is not a mechanism. A rhyme between vocabularies is not a correspondence. The Langlands program shines in **three specific mathematical structures**: **duality** (the dual group as a physical fact), **automorphy** (the spectral side — spectra organized by representation theory), and **correspondence** (the geometric program realized by a quantum field theory). Not the whole edifice, not every L-function — a specific, identifiable subclass.

What the program *is*, in one paragraph: the 1967 letter to Weil proposed the nonabelian generalization of class field theory. Class field theory identifies the abelian Galois groups of number fields with idele class characters — reciprocity perfected. Langlands' proposal: an $n$-dimensional Galois representation should match an automorphic representation of an adelic group $G(\mathbb{A})$, the match certified by equality of L-functions. The geometric version replaces number fields by curves: flat connections for the Langlands dual group $\,{}^LG$ on a curve $C$ should match Hecke-eigensheaves on the moduli of $G$-bundles over $C$. The program is called a grand unified theory of mathematics; this map asks the series' standing question — where does it *do physical work*?

One duality, three questions: *what is the magnetic description* (duality), *how are the spectra organized* (automorphy), *what matches the two sides* (correspondence).

The dividing line is this: **the Langlands program is present when a physical theory realizes the correspondence — when S-duality acts on a spectrum of line operators, when a partition function is an automorphic object, when an integrable system is a gauge theory. It is a lens when physics reads arithmetic — the trace formula as a path integral, zeros as energies, ensembles as symmetry types. It is silent where no realization exists — the number-field core, functoriality, the Riemann hypothesis.** The lens has teeth exactly where the structures coincide (Selberg); it is a metaphor everywhere else, and this map must keep that distinction sharp.

Two position notes. First: this is the only map in the series where the primary traffic runs **from physics into mathematics** — supersymmetric twists, ramification-as-defects, Coulomb branches, and category-theoretic renormalization were borrowed from physics and used to prove theorems, including the hardest ones. Second: this is the first map whose subject is a *program of conjectures* rather than a field, so "where it shines" must include, honestly marked, "where it is only aspiration." The historical rhyme is worth stating: Langlands' letter circulated in 1967; Goddard–Nuyts–Olive and Montonen–Olive conjectured the dual group in physics in 1977 — two programs, written a decade apart by different communities, meeting thirty years later in the twisted theory.

## 1. Duality (the dual group)

### What it is here

The Langlands dual group $\,{}^LG$ — roots and weights exchanged — is not a convention; in physics it is the group in which magnetic charge lives. Goddard–Nuyts–Olive observed that the charge lattice of a gauge theory with monopoles is naturally the weight lattice of a second group: the dual. Montonen–Olive made the conjecture that became the charter: in maximally supersymmetric Yang–Mills, the theory with group $G$ at coupling $\tau$ is *the same theory* as the theory with group $\,{}^LG$ at $\tau \to -1/\tau$ — electric-magnetic duality as an exact equivalence, with the full $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ acting on $\tau$.

### Four levels of duality content

**Level 1 — Abelian.** Maxwell's equations are self-dual under $\tau \to -1/\tau$ — the seed of everything. Class field theory is the abelian Langlands correspondence, and the abelian geometric case is the Fourier–Mukai transform: the self-duality of the Jacobian of a curve. **Proven**, and physically it is the electromagnetic duality of U(1) on a curve — the geometry map's periods, re-read.

**Level 2 — Nonabelian charge.** 't Hooft–Polyakov monopoles carry magnetic charge in the coweight lattice; GNO read off the dual group; Montonen–Olive conjectured that electric and magnetic sectors are one theory seen twice. The strong-coupling regime — unreachable by perturbation — is described *only* through the dual: the dual group is not decoration but the sole language of half the coupling space.

**Level 3 — The duality web.** Seiberg–Witten: the $\mathcal{N}=2$ Coulomb branch, its monodromies in $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$, the spectrum read from periods of the SW curve — the abelian geometric Langlands realized on a gauge-theory branch. Three-dimensional mirror symmetry is Langlands duality for 3d gauge theories ($G \leftrightarrow {}^LG$; the abelian case is a theorem of partition-function identities). Class S: the S-duality groups are mapping class groups. 3d bosonization weaves the same exchanges into duality webs.

**Level 4 — The exact identification.** Kapustin–Witten (2006): S-duality of $\mathcal{N}=4$ acts on the **spectrum of line operators** — Wilson operators (electric, $G$-data) dual to 't Hooft operators (magnetic, $\,{}^LG$-data) — and this action *is* the geometric Langlands correspondence, stated physically. The dual group is the group of the magnetic line operators: a physical observable, not a notation.

### The identification

| Physical structure | Langlands object | Identification |
|---|---|---|
| Maxwell self-duality | Abelian class field theory | $\tau \to -1/\tau$ |
| Montonen–Olive | $G \leftrightarrow {}^LG$ | The dual group as magnetic charge's group |
| SW monodromy | $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ on periods | Abelian geometric Langlands |
| 3d mirror symmetry | Langlands duality of 3d | Abelian case proven by partition functions |
| KW line operators | Wilson ↔ 't Hooft | The correspondence's physical face |

### Where duality shines

**Strongest case: S-duality of $\mathcal{N}=4$ as tested structure.** The duality survives precision tests — partition functions computed by localization are S-invariant, chiral observables transform correctly, the line-operator spectrum carries $G$ on one side and $\,{}^LG$ on the other. The dual group appears as the *structure of the strong-coupling description* — the one place in physics where a number-theoretic group redefinition is an exact physical symmetry.

**Second: Seiberg–Witten as abelian geometric Langlands.** The map's strongest moduli case (the geometry map's §3) is, in Langlands' language, the rank-one correspondence realized: monodromy = reciprocity, periods = the bridge.

**Third: 3d mirror symmetry.** The dual group exchange as a theorem-in-the-abelian-case and a working tool everywhere else — the duality web's daily currency.

### Where duality is not active

- QCD, the Standard Model, generic gauge theory: no S-duality is known; the dual group of QCD describes nothing measured.
- The duality is exact only under maximal supersymmetry or its topological twists — a feature of the sealed sector (§6), not of the unsealed bulk.

## 2. Automorphy (the spectral side)

### What it is here

Automorphic representations are the nonabelian generalization of Fourier modes — and spectra organized by them are the arithmetic map's strongest cases, *explained*. The modular surface's Maass spectrum, the Hecke eigenvalues, the arithmetic quantum chaos program: all are the GL(2) tip of the automorphic decomposition. Langlands is the statement that this organization is general — and physics has been reading the spectral side of the program since before the program had physics readers.

### Four levels of automorphic content

**Level 1 — The trace formula as quantum mechanics.** The Selberg trace formula is exactly a quantum-mechanical statement: the spectral side is the energy spectrum of a free particle on a hyperbolic quotient; the geometric side is the sum over closed geodesics — the classical periodic orbits. Witten has been developing the systematic reading: **the spectral side is the quantization, the geometric side is its semiclassical expansion, and the Langlands program's spectral questions become questions about quantization.** Arthur's trace formula generalizes; the founders' own comparison of orbital integrals to Feynman integrals is the same instinct.

**Level 2 — Hecke operators as the commuting algebra.** The Hecke eigenbasis is the "good basis" in which the arithmetic map's fingerprint was diagnosed — repulsion *and* degeneracy interlocked. And the deepest statistical fact: **Katz–Sarnak** — families of L-functions have symmetry types classified by classical groups, and the symmetry types are *the Wigner–Dyson ensembles*. The ensembles of quantum chaos govern the statistics of number theory, because the monodromy of a family of L-functions is a classical group. Random matrix theory, invented for the compound nucleus, reappears as the statistics of the Riemann zeta's nears and neighbors.

**Level 3 — Galois symmetry of thermodynamics.** Bost–Connes: a quantum statistical mechanical system whose partition function is $\zeta(\beta)$, with a phase transition at $\beta = 1$, and whose equilibrium (KMS) states are indexed by the number fields — with the absolute Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ acting as the symmetry, spontaneously broken at low temperature. The one physical system whose phases are number fields and whose broken symmetry is arithmetic: the arithmetic map's Level 4 speculation, given an exact home here as "Langlands for the adeles of $\mathbb{Q}$."

**Level 4 — Automorphic observables.** Supersymmetric localization delivers exact observables: the chiral correlators of the $\mathcal{N}=2^*$ theory are (quasi)modular forms — automorphic structure in measurable quantities, tested through S-duality. Faddeev's modular double — a quantum group tensored with its dual-parameter twin, $q$ paired with $-q^{-1}$ — realizes the S-duality $\tau \to -1/\tau$ at the representation level and underlies Liouville theory and 3d holomorphic blocks. The superconformal indices of 5d theories factorize over line operators with double-affine-Hecke-algebra structure: automorphic algebra in the observables.

### The identification

| Physical structure | Automorphic object | Identification |
|---|---|---|
| Modular surface spectrum | Maass forms, Hecke basis | The GL(2) tip |
| L-function families | Wigner–Dyson ensembles | Katz–Sarnak monodromy |
| Bost–Connes phases | Number fields; Galois action | Symmetry breaking = arithmetic |
| $\mathcal{N}=2^*$ observables | (Quasi)modular forms | Localization-exact |
| Liouville / 3d blocks | Faddeev's modular double | $\tau \to -1/\tau$ on representations |

### Where automorphy shines

**Strongest case: the trace formula as quantization.** For hyperbolic surfaces the dictionary is exact — the spectra map's treaty — and the Langlands program is the general theory of that treaty: the spectral side is the quantum theory, the geometric side its semiclassical shadow, and functoriality the statement about how spectra transfer. The arithmetic map's third-strongest case is here revealed as the local instance of a nonabelian harmonic analysis.

**Second: Katz–Sarnak.** The one place the borrowing is *quantitative*: the ensembles are the same, and the monodromy explains why. Physics' statistics of chaos is arithmetic's statistics of L-functions — one classification, two subjects.

**Third: Bost–Connes.** Exact, small, real: the Galois group as a spontaneously broken physical symmetry — arithmetic symmetry breaking, still the only one known.

### Where automorphy is not active

- Generic quantum spectra: no automorphic structure; the organization is special to arithmetic quotients and supersymmetric observables.
- The continuous spectrum of scattering; most of experimental physics.

## 3. Correspondence (the geometric match)

### What it is here

Geometric Langlands: on a curve $C$, flat $\,{}^LG$-connections correspond to Hecke-eigensheaves on the moduli stack of $G$-bundles — D-modules (or ind-coherent sheaves) whose eigenvalue under Hecke operators is the local system. It is the program's most theorem-shaped province, and the one realized by physics.

### Four levels of correspondence content

**Level 1 — Abelian: Fourier–Mukai.** Proven long ago, and physically it *is* electromagnetic duality of U(1) on a curve: the two sides are the self-dual Jacobian. The abelian case is the complete dictionary — and the geometry map's periods live here.

**Level 2 — Geometric Satake via monopoles.** The category on the affine Grassmannian of $G$ realizes $\mathrm{Rep}({}^LG)$ — the categorical engine. Physically: the affine Grassmannian is the geometry of monopole operators, and the Coulomb branch construction of 3d $\mathcal{N}=4$ gauge theories (Braverman–Finkelberg–Nakajima), distilled from 3d mirror symmetry, *supplied the algebraic definition* mathematicians then used. Instanton moduli (Nakajima quiver varieties) play the same role from the 4d side. Physics handed mathematics a tool.

**Level 3 — Kapustin–Witten.** The two topological twists of $\mathcal{N}=4$: the A-twist's Wilson operators carry D-module data; the B-twist's 't Hooft operators carry flat-connection data; **S-duality exchanges the twists**, and the statement that the brane categories match under the exchange is the geometric Langlands correspondence. Ramification becomes defects — monodromy at punctures; wild ramification, irregular singularities. Proposed as physics in 2006, the mathematical statements it organized were proven over the following decade and a half — the de Rham correspondence in generality by Gaitsgory, Raskin, and collaborators, with the proof methods themselves carrying physics' accent: categorical *renormalization*, induction on the curve, the constructions a field theory suggests. **The only case in the series where a deep mathematical correspondence was first articulated as physics and then proved as mathematics.**

**Level 4 — Quantum and holographic avatars.** Nekrasov–Shatashvili: the $\Omega$-background quantizes the Hitchin system, and the quantized spectral curve is the **oper** — Beilinson–Drinfeld's object — yielding the gauge/Bethe correspondence: supersymmetric gauge theories as integrable lattice systems, Bethe equations as vacuum equations, the Yangian (the algebra map's integrability) as the gauge-theory's symmetry. "Quantization commutes with Langlands" — theorem in cases, conjecture in general; quantum geometric Langlands proven for GL$_n$ (Gaitsgory–Lysenko). DAHA as the quantum Langlands algebra in 5d/6d indices. And the Betti variant: Hausel–Thaddeus showed mirror symmetry of Higgs-bundle moduli *is* Langlands duality ($G \leftrightarrow {}^LG$), and passed the topological mirror test by **counting points over finite fields** — the Weil conjectures certifying a statement about complex geometry. The P=W conjecture (perverse filtration = weight filtration on Higgs moduli) — now a theorem — is an arithmetic filtration surfacing inside gauge-theory geometry.

### The identification

| Physical structure | Correspondence object | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Wilson operators | Hecke eigensheaves | Proven (math) / exact (physics) |
| 't Hooft operators | Flat $\,{}^LG$ connections | Proven (math) / exact (physics) |
| Ramification | Defects, monodromy | Framework exact |
| Ω-background | Quantization → opers | Theorem in cases |
| Gauge/Bethe | Integrable systems as gauge theory | Theorem in classes |
| Higgs mirror pair | $G \leftrightarrow {}^LG$ | Proven via point counts |

### Where correspondence shines

**Strongest case: Kapustin–Witten.** The traffic direction is the point: physics proposed the framework, mathematics proved the correspondence, and physics gained the *meaning* of its own duality — S-duality is reciprocity, the local face of the program that unifies mathematics' dualities.

**Second: gauge/Bethe.** The most operational interface: integrable systems — the algebra map's measure-zero engine — realized as gauge vacua, with Bethe states computed by supersymmetric partition functions. Used daily in both communities.

**Third: geometric Satake via monopoles.** The 3d Coulomb branch as the affine Grassmannian: physics' monopole operators are the generators of the categorical engine.

**Fourth: arithmetic certifying geometry.** The mirror test for Higgs moduli passed by point-counting over finite fields — Deligne's theorem (the deepest arithmetic of the function-field world) verifying a mirror-symmetry statement. The two maps' flagship structures — mirror (geometry) and the Weil conjectures (arithmetic) — locking together, with Langlands duality as the lock.

### Where correspondence is not active

- No realization is known outside supersymmetric/topologically twisted theory; the unsealed bulk of QFT knows nothing of the correspondence.
- No Standard Model content; gravity only at the Chern–Simons/AdS₃ corners.

## 4. Where duality, automorphy, and correspondence meet

**Example 1 — $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM.** Duality: the $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ acting on $\tau$, exchanging $G$ and $\,{}^LG$. Correspondence: the KW identification of that action on line operators with geometric Langlands. Automorphy: the modular structure of its observables and the integrable spectral problem with its Yangian. The one theory carrying all three structures at maximum.

**Example 2 — The Hitchin fibration.** The integrable system whose spectral curves are the Seiberg–Witten curves (geometry and arithmetic's periods); whose quantization is the oper (Nekrasov–Shatashvili's correspondence); and — the summit — whose geometry supplied **Ngô's proof of the Fundamental Lemma (2008)**, the hardest theorem of the *arithmetic* Langlands program: endoscopy read as the support geometry of the same fibration gauge theory made central. One geometric object carrying physics' integrable systems and arithmetic's deepest step.

**Example 3 — The modular surface.** Automorphy: the Maass spectrum, Hecke-organized, the fingerprint of the arithmetic map. Correspondence: the Selberg trace formula as the exact spectrum-orbits dictionary. Duality: the Selberg zeta's zeros — which include, for BTZ black holes, the quasi-normal modes of the spectra map's ringdown. The smallest object that touches five of the nine maps.

**The pattern:** in each case the duality is the symmetry, the automorphy is the organization of the spectrum, and the correspondence is the *statement that the two descriptions match*. The Langlands program is the only structure in the series whose native claim is about **the agreement of descriptions** — which is why it is the reciprocity layer.

## 5. The frontier: the arithmetic half

The series' frontiers have been inversions — geometry as output, algebra as author, calculus as manufacturer of the discrete, topology as total classifier, spectra as spectrum-as-theory, complexity as interior-as-computation, degrees of freedom as deriving-the-inventory. The Langlands frontier is the **unfinished half**: the geometric correspondence is realized by physics; the arithmetic correspondence is not, and the frontier is making the lens into a mechanism.

- **A Hamiltonian for the correspondence.** What would count: a physical system whose spectrum is organized by the *global* Langlands match — not the Selberg model (which realizes the trace formula, not the Galois side). Connes' adelic program is the most serious attempt; the **Euler product obstruction** stands — the physical analogy reproduces functional equations but not the Euler product, which is the arithmetic content.
- **The Riemann hypothesis.** Hilbert–Pólya, Berry–Keating, Connes: unfulfilled. The honest lesson from the Selberg zeta: self-adjointness forces zeros onto the line *trivially* — the easy part is the line; the hard part is the Euler product. The wall between the analogy and the mechanism is precisely the arithmetic.
- **Functoriality as physics.** What operation on a quantum system transfers its spectrum functorially? No one knows — the question is well-posed and untouched.
- **Amplitudes as periods.** The live edge: loop amplitudes evaluate to periods — multiple zeta values at two and three loops, elliptic polylogs in the massive and elliptic sectors — and periods are the Galois side's currency. The motivic structure of amplitudes is the frontier where arithmetic Langlands might first earn *observational* content: Freund–Witten's dream, matured.
- **Quantization commutes with Langlands.** The slogan of Nekrasov–Shatashvili, theorem-in-cases: whether *quantization itself* is the physical content of the correspondence — the deepest question the program can ask physics.

**Status: PARTIAL for the geometric half — theorems with physics as scaffold. SPECULATIVE for the arithmetic half — mathematics and metaphors, no mechanism yet.**

## 6. The nonet interface

Six hinges:

1. **Arithmetic — the explained corners.** The arithmetic map's strongest cases are the GL(2) shadow of automorphy: modular invariance of CFT is automorphy for the modular group; the Maass spectrum is the Hecke basis; the four spectral levels (integer, algebraic, modular, L-function) ascend the Langlands hierarchy. And the honest subtraction: the nonabelian number-field core — the heart of the program — has no physics yet. This map is where the arithmetic map's speculative entries (Berry–Keating, Connes, Freund–Witten, Bost–Connes) find their systematic home.

2. **Geometry — mirror symmetry completed.** KW's A- and B-branes are a mirror pair on the Higgs moduli; Hausel–Thaddeus made mirror symmetry *be* Langlands duality and certified it by point-counting; the geometry map's strongest moduli case (Seiberg–Witten) is the abelian correspondence. Mirror and reciprocity are one structure seen from two maps.

3. **Algebra — the dual group's representations.** Affine Kac–Moody at critical level (Beilinson–Drinfeld), vertex algebras (the 4d/2d chiral algebra correspondence shares the machinery), DAHA as the quantum Langlands algebra, the Yangian on the gauge side of gauge/Bethe. The algebra map's moonshine-adjacent structures and this map's automorphic structures are one family.

4. **Calculus — the oper.** The automorphic side, quantized, becomes differential operators: opers are calculus objects on both ends. And the trace formula's geometric side is Gutzwiller's semiclassical expansion — the engine's own statistics, exact in the hyperbolic case.

5. **Topology — ramification as defects.** The correspondence lives in the topologically twisted sector: S-duality is exact *where the theory is sealed*. Ramification = monodromy defects; wild ramification = irregular singularities (the ADE country); and the BTZ ringdown read off the Selberg zeta ties the topology map's CS/AdS₃ corner to the spectra map's evidence.

6. **Spectra — the treaty, deepened into a program.** Selberg's exact dictionary becomes Arthur's trace formula and the model for functoriality: "how do spectra transfer?" is the spectral question the Langlands program asks on behalf of the spectra map — unanswered in general, asked now in the right language. And the RMT of L-functions (Katz–Sarnak) is the spectra map's fingerprint holding in arithmetic: the ensembles are universal across both subjects because monodromy is.

## 7. The dividing line

**The Langlands program is active when:**

- A physical theory realizes the correspondence: S-duality acting on line operators, brane categories matching under the exchange.
- The dual group is the structure of a description: magnetic charge's group, the 3d mirror, the monodromy of SW curves.
- Spectra are automorphic: trace formulas, Hecke bases, automorphic exact observables, gauge/Bethe.
- The statistics transfer: Katz–Sarnak's ensembles — physics' chaos statistics governing arithmetic's values.

**It is a lens when:**

- Physics reads arithmetic: the trace formula as a path integral, zeros as energies, the "Riemann gas," orbital integrals as Feynman integrals. Real analogies — some with theorems inside (Selberg) — but the physics is descriptive, not generative.

**It is silent when:**

- The question is the number-field core: no physical realization of global Langlands; no Hamiltonian; the Euler product obstruction.
- The question is functoriality as an operation on nature: well-posed, untouched.
- The question is the Standard Model: no Langlands content in masses, generations, or couplings — the numerological temptation, refused on every map.
- The question is probability: the Born rule — the wall all nine maps share. Langlands, the great matcher of descriptions, does not even approach it: it matches structures; it never weights outcomes.

The dividing line is **realization versus reading.** If a quantum field theory *is* one side of the correspondence and its duality *is* the match, the program is physics. If a physical system *models* the mathematics' spectral side, it is a lens — with teeth in proportion to exactness. If only the vocabulary is shared, it is silence with good manners.

Three distinctions carry the weight:

- **Realization ≠ analogy** (N=4 realizes; the Riemann gas does not).
- **The dual group ≠ a new force** (${}^LG$ is the structure of a description, not an interaction).
- **Automorphic ≠ modular** (modular forms are the GL(2) tip; automorphy is the nonabelian generalization — most of the program is not about the modular group at all).

## 8. Capacities and limits

### ✅ Five capacities

1. **The dual group as physical structure** — S-duality of $\mathcal{N}=4$, tested by localization; the line-operator spectrum carrying $G$ and $\,{}^LG$. Strongest: the one exact number-theoretic symmetry in physics' coupling space.
2. **The correspondence as physics** — Kapustin–Witten: proposed as a duality, proven as mathematics, and the proof methods carried physics' accent. The series' only physics→mathematics theorem pipeline of this depth.
3. **The spectral side as mechanics** — the trace formula as quantization (exact for hyperbolic surfaces; programmatic beyond); Bost–Connes' Galois symmetry breaking; Katz–Sarnak's ensembles.
4. **Integrability as gauge theory** — gauge/Bethe, opers as quantum spectral curves, the modular double: the algebra map's engine given a Langlands-dual home.
5. **Arithmetic certifying geometry** — the mirror test passed by point-counting; Ngô's Fundamental Lemma through the Hitchin fibration; P=W. The program's mathematics standing on physics' objects.

### ❌ Four silences

1. **No realization of the arithmetic half.** The global correspondence — the program's heart — has no physical system. The lens is good; the mechanism is missing.
2. **The Riemann hypothesis is not physics.** Hilbert–Pólya unfulfilled; the Euler product obstruction exact; the line was never the hard part.
3. **No Langlands content in generic physics.** QCD, the Standard Model, gravity beyond the CS corners: the program describes the sealed sector of special theories, and functoriality has no operation in nature.
4. **No claim on probability.** The Born rule stands outside all nine maps — matched descriptions, unweighted outcomes.

### ⚠️ Three boundary cases

1. **Quantum Langlands / gauge–Bethe.** Theorems in classes, conjecture in general — the most active technical frontier. Status: PARTIALLY APPLICABLE.
2. **Amplitudes as periods.** MZVs and elliptic structures observed in loops; the motivic program open; observational content not yet earned. Status: PARTIALLY APPLICABLE.
3. **The lens's extent.** How far the trace-formula-as-quantization reading extends (Arthur's formula, functoriality) is Witten's open program: the lens may yet focus. Status: PARTIALLY APPLICABLE.
