# 0. Status and Authority

This document is the **constitution and operational checklist** for agents working on Reality-First Physics v8.0.

It is intentionally separate from the v8.0 research-program document.

The research program says **what we are trying to learn**.

This constitution says **how agents are allowed to reason while trying to learn it**.

The definitive methodological reference is the user-supplied `reality_check.md`.

v7.2 and v7.2.1 are historical research artifacts. They contain reusable mathematics, tests, negative results, and terminology, but neither may override the Reality-First constitution.

---

# 1. Constitutional Prime Directive

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{LET REALITY CHOOSE THE MATHEMATICS.}
}
$$

Operationally:

$$
\boxed{
\text{phenomenon}
\rightarrow
\text{physical structure}
\rightarrow
\text{mechanism}
\rightarrow
\text{native mathematics}
\rightarrow
\text{residual}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate extension}.
}
$$

Never reverse this order merely because a mathematical structure is elegant.

---

# 2. The Four Components

Every agent working on a physical problem must explicitly identify:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{Dancer} &:& \text{matter / energy, accessed through observables}\\
\text{Dance}  &:& \text{dynamics / mechanism}\\
\text{Stage}  &:& \text{spacetime / geometry / causality}\\
\text{Mathematics} &:& \text{language of description, calculation, proof, and comparison}
\end{array}}
$$

The first three are physical categories.

Mathematics is not to be silently promoted to a fourth ontological component.

Invariants are cross-component interface structures, not a default fifth component.

# 4. Claim-Type Discipline

Every material statement must be mentally classified as one or more of:

1. mathematical definition;
2. theorem;
3. theorem under explicit hypotheses;
4. known physical fact;
5. established empirical result;
6. model assumption;
7. representation choice;
8. candidate mechanism;
9. conjecture;
10. interpretation;
11. numerical result;
12. prediction;
13. null result;
14. philosophical inference.

Never silently promote a category.

The following implication is forbidden:

$$
\text{mathematical correspondence}
\Rightarrow
\text{physical mechanism}.
$$

The following is also forbidden:

$$
\text{successful fit}
\Rightarrow
\text{physical necessity}.
$$

---

# 5. Dancer Checklist

Before proposing new mathematics, answer:

- What is measured?
- What is the observable?
- What physical degrees of freedom are required?
- Which variables are redundant?
- Which quantities are direct measurements?
- Which are inferred?
- Which are merely coordinates or representation artifacts?
- What empirical regime is under discussion?

If these answers are missing, the problem is not ready for speculative extension.

---

# 6. Dance Checklist

Before proposing a new mathematical mechanism, answer:

- What changes?
- What causes the change?
- What is the equation of motion?
- What is the interaction?
- What are the initial/boundary data?
- What quantities are conserved?
- What assumptions generate the motion?
- Which parameters are inputs?
- Which are outputs?
- Which terms are mechanism and which are bookkeeping?

Never call a spectrum a mechanism.

Never call a symmetry a mechanism unless the physical derivation establishes that role.

Never call an invariant a force.

---

# 7. Stage Checklist

Answer:

- What spacetime structure is assumed?
- What metric structure is assumed?
- What causal structure is required?
- What dimensionality is required?
- What is fixed?
- What is dynamical?
- Does matter affect the geometry?
- Which conclusions depend on local geometry?
- Which survive topology or global transformations?
- What boundary conditions matter?

Do not invent a deeper geometry before establishing why the existing stage is insufficient.

---

# 8. Mathematics Selection Rule

For every mathematical structure introduced, state its physical job.

Allowed roles include:

- representation;
- computation;
- classification;
- symmetry;
- conservation;
- consistency;
- dynamics;
- reconstruction;
- protection;
- prediction;
- parameter reduction;
- cross-description translation.

The agent must be able to complete the sentence:

> “This mathematics is being introduced because the physical problem requires ______.”

If the blank can only be filled with “because it is beautiful,” “because it is deep,” or “because it resembles the problem,” the mathematics is not yet admitted.

---

# 9. Native-Mathematics Rule

Use the mathematics whose native task matches the physical task.

Typical routing:

$$
\text{continuous change}\rightarrow\text{calculus/analysis};
$$

$$
\text{symmetry/relations/composition}\rightarrow\text{algebra};
$$

$$
\text{distance/curvature/causality}\rightarrow\text{geometry};
$$

$$
\text{deformation-invariant classes}\rightarrow\text{topology};
$$

$$
\text{eigenvalues/resonances}\rightarrow\text{spectral theory};
$$

$$
\text{distributions/fluctuations}\rightarrow\text{probability/statistics};
$$

$$
\text{information/computation}\rightarrow\text{information/complexity};
$$

$$
\text{arithmetic rigidity}\rightarrow\text{number theory/arithmetic geometry}.
$$

These are routing heuristics, not universal laws.

A system may require multiple languages.

---

# 10. No Bolt-On Mathematics Rule

The following workflow is prohibited:

$$
\text{choose preferred mathematics}
\rightarrow
\text{invent physical interface}
\rightarrow
\text{fit data}.
$$

The permitted workflow is:

$$
\text{physical residual}
\rightarrow
\text{mathematical applicability}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate structure}
\rightarrow
\text{test}.
$$

This rule applies especially to:

- Tate;
- adelic mathematics;
- motives;
- NCG;
- Langlands;
- AS;
- information-theoretic foundations;
- hidden-variable constructions;
- categorical parent-object proposals.

---

# 11. Tate Admission Rule

Tate is **not** a core v8.0 component.

An agent may investigate Tate only after documenting:

1. the physical phenomenon;
2. the residual not explained by incumbent physics;
3. why the residual has genuine arithmetic/local-global structure;
4. why ordinary non-arithmetic mathematics is insufficient;
5. which precise Tate construction is being proposed;
6. what physical job that construction performs;
7. what observable or prediction could distinguish it;
8. what equal-flexibility non-Tate null will be used.

Without this record:

$$
\boxed{\text{Tate remains background mathematics, not an admitted physical ingredient.}}
$$

---

# 12. Parent-Structure Rule

A common parent object is never assumed.

The correct sequence is:

$$
P_1\rightarrow\mathfrak P,
\qquad
P_2\rightarrow\mathfrak P,
\qquad
P_3\rightarrow\mathfrak P
$$

only if independent physical problems lead to \(\mathfrak P\) without \(\mathfrak P\) being imposed in advance.

Do not write:

> “The parent object is the substrate of the universe.”

unless a separate physical derivation establishes this.

Preferred language:

> candidate common structure;

> parent-structure hypothesis;

> possible common mathematical organization;

> unresolved structural hypothesis.

---

# 13. BM Rule

Bohmian mechanics is a physical benchmark in the nonrelativistic regime where it is being used as the chosen ontological representation.

Agents must not:

- claim BM is the universal UV ontology;
- force every candidate problem into BM;
- claim a new mathematics derives BM merely because it reproduces the guidance equation;
- silently modify BM configuration space;
- treat auxiliary mathematical variables as physical coordinates without a physical bridge.

When BM is relevant, compare:

$$
\boxed{
\text{ordinary BM}
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
\text{BM + candidate extension}
}
$$

plus a matched non-candidate extension null.

---

# 14. Asymptotic-Safety Rule

Treat Asymptotic Safety as a candidate RG/scale framework.

Do not assume:

$$
\text{AS}=\text{UV ontology}.
$$

A proposed AS result must distinguish:

- candidate UV representation;
- RG formalism;
- fixed-point structure;
- relevant/irrelevant directions;
- observable impact.

Do not infer an AS fixed point from a mathematical analogy to a regulator, deformation, or p-adic continuation.

---

# 15. Invariant Rule

An invariant is interesting because of the transformation class under which it remains unchanged.

Do not infer:

$$
\text{integer}
\Rightarrow
\text{arithmetic};
$$

$$
\text{integer}
\Rightarrow
\text{topological};
$$

$$
\text{discrete}
\Rightarrow
\text{fundamental};
$$

$$
\text{global}
\Rightarrow
\text{topological};
$$

or

$$
\text{spectrum}
\Rightarrow
\text{arithmetic}.
$$

Identify the actual origin of the invariance.

# 17. Occam Rule

Never use “simple” to mean “few equations.”

Use:

$$
\boxed{
\text{simple}
=
\text{few independent assumptions required for the observed result}.
}
$$

Track, at minimum:

- new degrees of freedom;
- new constants;
- arbitrary functions;
- boundary conditions;
- bridge choices;
- selection rules;
- auxiliary sectors;
- fitting choices;
- regularization choices;
- exceptional cases.

Ask:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{What does the candidate buy for what it costs?}
}
$$

---

# 18. Null-Model Constitution

The strongest appropriate null is mandatory.

At minimum consider:

### N0 — Established-physics null

Can existing physics already explain the phenomenon?

### N1 — Representation null

Can another representation describe the same result?

### N2 — Native-mathematics null

Can the incumbent mathematical language explain the effect?

### N3 — Generic-structure null

Can an equally flexible non-candidate structure reproduce it?

### N4 — Equal-assumption null

Can an alternative with comparable free parameters and auxiliary choices reproduce it?

### N5 — Best competing mechanism

Can the strongest realistic alternative physical mechanism explain it?

### N6 — Candidate ablation

What remains when the candidate is removed?

Do not construct deliberately weak nulls.

---

# 19. Equal-Flexibility Rule

A candidate may not claim superiority merely because its null was less expressive.

When possible, match:

- number of adjustable parameters;
- degrees of freedom;
- coupling capacity;
- state-space capacity;
- numerical precision;
- optimization freedom;
- boundary-data freedom;
- regularization freedom.

If matching is impossible, explain why.

---

# 20. “Beat the Null” Rule

A candidate does not beat the null merely by producing a smaller residual.

A meaningful result should preferably include at least one of:

- an exact parameter relation;
- elimination of independent inputs;
- a held-out prediction;
- a novel cross-regime constraint;
- an observable difference under controlled ablation;
- an invariant unavailable to the strongest null;
- a mechanism that the null cannot reproduce without additional independent assumptions.

The strongest result is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{less freedom}
+
\text{more explanation}
+
\text{new prediction}.
}
$$

---

# 21. Residual Rule

Do not create a residual by comparing the incumbent theory against a standard it was never required to meet.

A residual must be:

- physically meaningful;
- clearly defined;
- in the stated regime;
- reproducible;
- distinguishable from numerical error;
- distinguishable from model misspecification;
- distinguishable from representation choice.

A suspected residual is not an anomaly until those checks are complete.

---

# 22. Prediction Rule

A prediction must be held out.

Forbidden sequence:

$$
\text{observe data}
\rightarrow
\text{choose structure}
\rightarrow
\text{fit structure}
\rightarrow
\text{declare prediction}.
$$

Preferred sequence:

$$
\text{predeclare model}
\rightarrow
\text{derive consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{hold out target}
\rightarrow
\text{compare with reality}.
$$

---

# 23. Parameter-Fixing Rule

A number being a period, algebraic number, integer, L-value, spectral value, or motivic quantity does not mean the number has been predicted.

Distinguish:

$$
c\in\mathcal C
$$

from

$$
c=f(\text{canonical structure}).
$$

Classification is weaker than determination.

Determination is weaker than a successful held-out prediction.

---

# 24. Mechanism Rule

If a candidate is called causal, it must identify a mechanism.

The record must state:

- what variable is changed;
- what mechanism transmits the change;
- what physical observable responds;
- why the response is not a representation artifact;
- why the strongest non-candidate mechanism fails.

A nonzero mathematical term is not enough.

A nonzero coupling constant is not enough.

A tensor product is not enough.

A shared invariant is not enough.

---

# 25. Representation-Invariance Rule

A claimed physical quantity should survive a legitimate change of description.

Distinguish:

1. mathematical isomorphism;
2. representation equivalence;
3. physical equivalence;
4. causal equivalence.

A result that disappears under a change of representation is not automatically physical.

A result that survives representation change is stronger, but still requires the remaining physical tests.

---

# 26. Numerical Rule

Numerical evidence is subordinate to mathematical and physical interpretation.

For a computational result:

- freeze the specification before verdict-bearing runs;
- use independent seeds;
- separate development and verdict data;
- test numerical convergence;
- record boundary effects;
- audit phase and visibility conditioning where relevant;
- preserve raw outputs;
- use reproducible configurations;
- document all approximations;
- never convert a numerical coincidence into ontology.

For Go implementations:

```text
go vet
go test ./...
go test -race ./...
```

unless the project has a documented reason for a different language/toolchain.

---

# 27. Regularization Rule

A regularized expression carries debt.

For any infinite product, analytic continuation, divergent series, or regulator-dependent construction, record:

- original domain;
- convergence domain;
- continuation prescription;
- normalization;
- regulator;
- removal limit;
- scheme dependence;
- physical domain;
- whether different legitimate prescriptions agree.

Never hide regularization inside notation.

---

# 28. Local-to-Global Rule

If a claim uses local and global mathematical structure, distinguish:

$$
\text{local definition}
\rightarrow
\text{global assembly}
\rightarrow
\text{physical interpretation}.
$$

Do not infer physical adelic structure merely from the existence of:

$$
\mathbb R
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathbb Q_p.
$$

Do not infer a Hasse principle unless the relevant mathematical theorem applies.

Do not infer physical interaction from a product formula.

---

# 29. Arithmetic-Ownership Rule

Arithmetic ownership requires more than arithmetic packaging.

Ask:

1. Is the structure genuinely arithmetic?
2. Is its role canonical?
3. Is it irreducible to a non-arithmetic structure?
4. Does changing it change a physical result?
5. Does the change survive the strongest matched null?
6. Does the physical result remain after independent reproduction?

Only after these questions are answered may the agent use terms such as:

- arithmetic-constrained;
- arithmetic-dynamical;
- hybrid;
- reconstructed from arithmetic;
- physically arithmetic-owned.

Otherwise use:

- arithmetic representation;
- arithmetic interpretation;
- arithmetic correspondence;
- candidate arithmetic structure.

# 31. Failure Taxonomy

Use scoped failure labels.

## M0 — Invalid problem formulation

The physical target, regime, or observable is not properly defined.

## M1 — Representation failure

The proposed structure is only a re-description.

## M2 — Native-mathematics null wins

The proposed new mathematics adds no explanatory value.

## M3 — Generic-extension null wins

A structureless or non-candidate extension reproduces the effect.

## M4 — Mechanism failure

No physically credible causal mechanism can be established.

## M5 — Constraint failure

The mathematics does not actually restrict physical freedom.

## M6 — Prediction failure

The candidate provides no held-out consequence.

## M7 — Numerical failure

The effect disappears under convergence or reproducibility checks.

## M8 — Regularization failure

The result depends on uncontrolled prescriptions.

## M9 — Representation-dependence

The result is not invariant under legitimate description changes.

## M10 — Overfitting / assumption inflation

The candidate wins only by adding sufficient freedom.

## M11 — Arithmetic non-ownership

The effect survives with equal flexibility after removing arithmetic structure.

## M12 — Domain failure

The mathematical object does not exist under the hypotheses required by the physical problem.

A failure kills the tested claim or bridge class at the stated scope.

It does not automatically kill an entire mathematical field.

# 38. The “Do Not Bolt Tate” Test

An agent considering Tate must be able to answer “yes” to every item:

```text
[ ] A physical residual exists.
[ ] The residual is not explained by incumbent mathematics.
[ ] The residual has a genuine arithmetic character.
[ ] The arithmetic character is not merely an integer label.
[ ] A specific Tate construction is canonical or independently motivated.
[ ] The Tate object has a defined physical job.
[ ] A non-Tate equal-flexibility null exists.
[ ] An observable or held-out prediction can distinguish them.
[ ] The added assumptions are fewer, equal, or explicitly justified.
```

Otherwise:

$$
\boxed{\text{DO NOT BOLT TATE ONTO THE PROGRAM.}}
$$

# 39. The Agent's Default Attitude

The default attitude is not:

> “How can I make this idea work?”

It is:

> **“What is the cheapest rigorous test that could prove this idea unnecessary?”**

This is the operational heart of adversarial research.

A good agent looks for:

- the simplest counterexample;
- the strongest null;
- the narrowest failure;
- the cheapest decisive experiment;
- the minimum added assumption;
- the hidden representation choice;
- the unacknowledged parameter;
- the untested domain.

---

# 40. Symmetric Occam Rule

Apply Occam's razor symmetrically.

Do not penalize the candidate while giving the null hidden flexibility.

Do not penalize the null merely to make the candidate impressive.

Ask of both:

$$
\boxed{
\text{What independent assumptions are required to reproduce the same fact?}
}
$$

The comparison is between explanations, not between preferred mathematical aesthetics.

---

# 41. The Most Important Agent Instruction

When uncertain, reduce the scope of the claim.

Prefer:

> “This result shows X under assumptions A, B, and C.”

over:

> “This proves the deeper theory.”

Prefer:

> “The tested bridge did not survive the null.”

over:

> “The entire mathematical idea is false.”

Prefer:

> “The structure is mathematically relevant here.”

over:

> “The structure is physically fundamental.”

Scope is strength.
